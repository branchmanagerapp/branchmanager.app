⚠️ Reality check before the math: the trucks that were "good shape, lower miles, made sense for us" were about $90k (Doug, Sept 10). A Class 5 hook-lift (F-550 / Ram 5500, 19,500 GVWR) or an F-650 spec'd under 26,000 GVWR does not need a CDL to drive. What does trigger one is towing: a 5500 pulling the 16–20 ft trailer with both machines is over 26,001 lb combined with a trailer over 10,000 lb, and that is CDL-A under the federal rule (confirm NY). The Ram 2500 pulling that same trailer stays under the line. A hook-lift is also not an errand or estimate truck. Decide who drives what before the math says yes. Financing a used truck for the LLC (no SBA): banks and credit unions want the truck under ~6 years and 75k miles. Equipment-finance lenders don't care about age: Crest Capital (no age or mileage limit, 24–72 mo, app-only under $250k) and Ameris/Balboa (1 yr in business, FICO 620, up to $500k) are the realistic doors. Expect 8–12% over 48–60 months with 10–20% down. A 2012 F-550 is financeable there; a 2004 is a cash truck.

The honest gut-check

The numbers still say consolidate over keep-all, but it is no longer free money. At $90k, after ~$14k from the F550 and a ~$3.6k check to clear the Ram, you finance roughly $80k. At 10% over 5 years that is about $1,700/mo against ~$1,620/mo you shed (Ram note, its insurance, F550 upkeep, disposal), plus ~$600/mo to run the hook-lift. Net is around −$680/mo before new work. It only pays if the hook-lift bills: container hauls, storm debris at Matt's "30-yard dump trailer" scale, and the days you currently rent or sub a truck. Set the extra-revenue box honestly and watch the verdict.

On the F550 price: a 2004 F-550 dump with 82k miles sold at municipal auction for $13,300 (Sept 2024); 2003–06 F-550s list $8–25k depending on body and miles; a 2008 chip truck at 151k asks $20k. A buyer prices a 2004 off condition and documented work (receipts, drivetrain, rebuild), not effort. $14k is the honest default; $18k with paperwork and a clean bed.

The thing the math can't see: one hook-lift can't be everywhere. If you regularly need a chipper running while a truck hauls, or someone running estimates, one truck won't cover it and you'll re-buy a pickup, wiping out the savings. And the sequence matters with $7.4k in the bank: the F550 sale and the Ram sale come first, the hook-lift purchase second, never the other way around. The $9,483 GL renewal on 9/18 comes before any of it.

The "cheaped out" lesson, said plainly: the Ram was the $40k answer to a $90k question, and 12.9% financing turned it into a $60k truck by 2031. The fix is not another compromise truck. It is either the right truck financed properly by a lender that will touch it, or keeping the Ram until it is paid down enough to leave clean (late 2027 at the current pace).
