What it actually earns (Sept 10 2026)

Doug's question: "one storm, it could probably pay for the truck, or two." Here is what that looks like in the three lanes a hook-lift can work, with the rate source for each. Truck cost assumed $90k at 12% over 5 years = ~$2,000/mo, ~$24k/yr (Doug: "my credit was shit on that", so no 8% fantasy).

Lane 1 · Insurance / restoration T&M under a packager (the Roscoe lane)

Rate basis is real: Servpro approved "pickup + dump trailer" at $350/hr and labor at $350/person/hr, portal-to-portal, 12.5-hr days. A hook-lift with a 30-yd container is at least that class. Billed to the prime: ~$8,750/day. Your share is the estimate: at Roscoe you received 40% of your own lines.

Answer in this lane: one 10-day deployment covers 1.5–2 years of truck payments. Two 10-day deployments pay for the truck. One Roscoe-size 4-day storm pays about 7–10 months of it. This is the lane Andrew works and the one Matt described ("power line jobs", "production cutting"). It only exists if the hook-lift is on the approved rate schedule before day one, which is what the rate schedule is for.

Lane 2 · FEMA-style per-cubic-yard debris hauling (the lane to avoid)

Published prime rates 2024–26 run $6.75–9.95/cy to the debris site (TFR, AshBritt, CrowderGulf, Southern Disaster Recovery contracts); FEMA calls $15/cy "reasonable." Subs see roughly 55–75% of that, and public T&M for a dump truck with driver on those contracts is $75–155/hr, not $350. A 30-yd hook-lift with an 85% load call carries ~25 cy and needs the prime's loader, so it waits in line behind self-loading grapple trucks.

Lane 3 · Residential, at home (what the Ram will never do)

A 30-yd roll-off pull in Peekskill is worth $609–782 to a homeowner (Eastchester $704–754). Tipping at Rockland Green: brush and chips $57/ton, clean chipped wood free, stumps $150/ton. So one can of chips costs $0–400 to dump and is worth ~$700 as a service. A local hauling day, 3–4 pulls, is ~$2,100–2,800 gross if you sell it as a service; on your own jobs it replaces the sub crane/log-truck line ($667/mo in 2025), the disposal line ($115/mo), and the second trip when the F-550 and a trailer both go out. Realistic shed on your own work: $400–800/mo. It does not carry a $2,000 payment alone, but it means the truck is never idle between storms, which the Ram is.

The straight answer: the hook-lift pays for itself in two 10-day insurance deployments at Roscoe-class rates, or one 10-day deployment plus a year of residential hauling. With the F550 staying, the monthly gap to cover is ~$1,270, about four Lane-1 storm days a year. It does not pay for itself in FEMA per-yard debris, and residential alone covers maybe a third of the payment. Buy it for Lane 1, keep it busy with Lane 3, say no to Lane 2. Everything in Lane 1 depends on the roster, the paper, and being on the call list, which is the Storm Playbook.

Path A · Transfer the truck to Doug personally, then refinance

The whole question is New York sales tax. If the DMV treats you taking over the loan as "consideration" for a sale from the LLC to you, tax at 8.375% (Putnam) on $37,609 is ~$3,150, plus ~$250 title and registration. Net over five years: ~$1,900 at 7.5%, ~$860 at 8.5%, a loss at 9.5% or above. If the transfer is exempt, the full savings above apply. Two more costs that ride along: the S-corp distributing a depreciated truck can be a deemed sale at market value inside the corporation (Kevin question), and a work truck on a personal auto policy is a coverage problem unless it carries a business-use endorsement or a commercial policy in your name.

Verifying now against the NY DTF-802/803 forms and current lender rules: whether the LLC-to-member transfer is taxable, which lenders refinance at ~112% loan-to-value, and their rates. This line updates when that lands.

Path B · Unsecured personal loan pays off Chase; the LLC keeps the title

No transfer, no sales tax, no retitle, no insurance change. You borrow personally, the new lender wires Chase, Chase releases the lien, the LLC owns the truck free and clear, and the books carry it as a shareholder loan the company repays you monthly. At 8–9% unsecured this is a $3,200–4,300 save with zero transfer cost. It is the path the people you talked to did not mention, and it only depends on your personal credit today, not the truck. Which lenders do this and at what rate is in the verification above.

Path C · No refinance: a $5,000 lump on principal

Saves $3,632 in interest and ends the loan 10 months early, keeping 12.9%. That is roughly a 9.5% refinance's benefit with no paperwork. Storm-check money does this. Every additional $5,000 does about the same again.

Path D · Keep it in the business name and shop commercial lenders

Crest Capital and Ameris/Balboa refinance commercial vehicles, but at 8–12% on a truck that is 110–115% of its value, the realistic offer is 10–12%, which saves $1,000–1,600 over five years. Not worth the closing costs. Doug's experience matches: nobody does it at a decent rate in the business name.

Ranking, pending the tax answer: Path B (unsecured personal, LLC keeps title) if your credit prices under ~9%; else Path C (lump principal from the next storm check); Path A only if the LLC-to-member transfer is confirmed sales-tax-exempt and the rate is under 8.5%; Path D not worth it. And none of this changes the hook-lift decision above: the Ram at any rate is a commute, not a revenue truck.

The honest gut-check

With the F550 staying, the numbers say sell the Ram rather than keep it, but the hook-lift is a revenue bet, not a savings move. At $90k plus a ~$3.6k check to clear the Ram, you finance roughly $93.6k. At 10% over 5 years that is about $1,990/mo against ~$1,320/mo you shed (Ram note, its insurance, disposal), plus ~$600/mo to run the hook-lift. Net is around −$1,270/mo before new work, versus −$2,200/mo if you keep the Ram too. It pays only if the hook-lift bills: insurance deployments (Lane 1 above), container hauls, and the days you now sub a truck. Set the extra-revenue box honestly and watch the verdict.

On the F550: a 2004 F-550 dump with 82k miles sold at municipal auction for $13,300 (Sept 2024); 2003–06 F-550s list $8–25k. Doug's call is right: a paid-off chip truck that runs is worth more to the business than $14k in cash. It stays, and it keeps the crew rolling on the days the hook-lift is on a deployment.

The thing the math can't see: one hook-lift can't be everywhere. If you regularly need a chipper running while a truck hauls, or someone running estimates, one truck won't cover it and you'll re-buy a pickup, wiping out the savings. And the sequence matters with $7.4k in the bank: the Ram sale comes first, the hook-lift purchase second, never the other way around. The $9,483 GL renewal on 9/18 comes before any of it.

The "cheaped out" lesson, said plainly: the Ram was the $40k answer to a $90k question, and 12.9% financing turned it into a $60k truck by 2031. The fix is not another compromise truck. It is either the right truck financed properly by a lender that will touch it, or keeping the Ram until it is paid down enough to leave clean (late 2027 at the current pace).
