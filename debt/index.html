The debt decision — straight, for me

What paying it off actually costs me, what bankruptcy actually does, and which path leaves me in the best spot.

The situation, no spin: ~$160k SBA loan in 2nd Nature 3, my personal guarantee on it, business failing, Jon willing to help carry it / take it over. This page is me thinking it through on my own terms — not a pitch to anyone. Not legal advice; Todd makes the final call.

Is paying it off actually best for me? (the real question)

"Jon and I just pay it" sounds like the responsible move. For the business it is. For me personally, it cuts both ways — and the catch is big.

Ways paying it IS best for me

  • No second bankruptcy on my record (I already used one). Protects my credit and future borrowing.
  • I keep the business as an asset + my income, and any upside if it turns around.
  • It's cheap money (3.75%) — not worth blowing up if it can genuinely be carried.
  • If Jon really shares the load, the monthly isn't all on me.

Ways it's NOT — and these are heavy

  • I stay personally on the hook the whole time. The guarantee doesn't end until it's fully paid — potentially years with my name on a failing business's debt.
  • "Jon pays it" = Jon pays MY guarantee. If he stops or it folds, it's my credit and my $160k. Liability without control.
  • The business is already failing — paying down debt on it can be good money after bad.
  • It keeps me financially tangled in it instead of a clean break to rebuild.
The core trade-off: paying it keeps me exposed; discharging it sets me free. So "pay it off" is only best for me if (a) the business is truly viable, and (b) I get off the guarantee soon — Jon refinancing it into his own name, or a fast payoff — not a slow 30-year carry with my name on it. Liability without control, on a business someone else runs, is the worst seat in the house.

The three paths — what each does for ME

A

Carry / pay it off (no bankruptcy) — ~$741/mo, or faster

Keep the loan and pay it. Business continues. But I stay personally guaranteed until it's gone — open-ended exposure. Best ONLY if the business is genuinely viable and I get released soon (Jon assumes it, or it's paid off fast). A 30-year slow-pay leaves me exposed for decades.

For me: low if released soon / high if I stay guarantor on a failing biz
B

Chapter 13 — pay a capped plan, then I'm FREE

A court plan (like my last one — $464/mo for 3 years). I pay the collateral value + what I can for 3–5 years, the rest is discharged, and critically my guarantee ends when the plan completes — capped, not open-ended. Keeps the business in play. Catch: I have to be able to fund the plan (~$1,283–2,038/mo here, above what the business shows now) and it's a second filing.

For me: caps my exposure and ends the guarantee on a deadline
C

Chapter 7 — clean break, $0, debt gone

Discharge the guarantee entirely. No payments. I surrender the corp's gear (Jon can buy it / take the business separately). Best for my own fresh start if the business is truly done. Likely qualifies easily (primarily business debt → may skip the means test). Catch: second bankruptcy on record; I give up the business.

For me: the cleanest personal exit — if I'm willing to let the business go
Read for my situation (failing + Jon taking over): if Jon runs it going forward, the move that protects me is to get off the guarantee — either Jon assumes/refinances the loan in his name (releasing me), or I discharge it (Ch.7), or I cap it and end it via Ch.13. The one path that's worst for me is the one that sounds nicest: "we'll just pay it" while my name stays on it for years and Jon holds the wheel.

The SBA reality (why the details matter)

It's an SBA loan, so: cheap (~$741/mo on 30-yr terms — carryable), but it doesn't settle like a normal creditor (government collection, lien on the corp's gear, formal Offer-in-Compromise only).

The lien sits on ~$68k of equipment — that's the "keep it" price in a Ch.13 cramdown, NOT the full $160k. And any hand-off to Jon has to be fair value, arm's-length, and sequenced by the attorney — moving assets cheap to an insider before filing gets clawed back and can cost the discharge. Clean or not at all.

Where this stands

My read: "pay it off" only wins for me if I'm not left holding the guarantee. If the business is really failing and Jon's taking over, the personally-cleanest moves are Ch.7 (free and clear) or, if I want to keep a hand in it, Ch.13 (capped, ends the guarantee on a clock). Carrying it open-ended is the trap.

The decision hinges on two numbers: my real disposable income (can I fund a plan?) and whether Jon will/can take the loan into his own name (which is what actually frees me). Both go to Todd.
