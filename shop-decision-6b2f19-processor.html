Revised position

You were right about the money, and right about the round trip.

Helcim saves $1,757/yr in processing on your real volume, and it holds up even if their rate lands far worse than advertised. The server round trip I proposed was an artifact of choosing Square — not good architecture. One narrow thing still stands, and it is not about whether skateOS is good.

Why it fits your shop specifically

Your average ticket is $26.08. The fixed fee dropping 15¢ to 8¢ is worth $479/yr on its own, across 6,838 transactions. Small tickets punish per-transaction fees, and you have a lot of them. Interchange-plus suits your mix better than flat-rate does — that is a structural advantage, not a promotional one.

2. The round trip was my mistake, not a requirement

The proxy existed for exactly one reason: Square has no browser-safe credential. A Square access token grants full account power — read customers, create charges, issue refunds, see payouts. It can never touch page JavaScript. So every request has to detour through a server that holds it.

Supabase is the opposite. Its anon key is designed to be public, with row-level security as the boundary. That is why your shop page calls it directly today with no server in between.

So the conclusion flips

The round trip was an artifact of choosing Square, not good practice. With skateOS as the back end it is browser → Supabase, exactly as it works now: no proxy, no detour, fewer moving parts.

My Square-backed design was the clunkier one — and it was clunkier because Square was never built to power someone else’s storefront. skateOS was.

3. Where I still hold — and it is narrow

Do not put an untested register in front of a Saturday crowd.

Not because skateOS is bad. Because the risk is asymmetric. $3,545/yr is about $10 a day. One broken Saturday with a line out the door costs more than that in walked sales and goodwill.

And my “0 orders” argument was weak in one direction

It will always be 0 until someone uses it. That is a reason to run a controlled test, not to avoid it forever. I stated it as though it were a permanent disqualifier. It is not.

4. The option I did not give you

Two decisions are being bundled that are actually separable:

  • Saving on fees is a processor decision.
  • Trusting skateOS is a software decision.

Path B captures your entire savings argument without the risk argument. If fees are what is driving this, you can have them without betting the register on code we wrote.

Note that leaving Square necessarily changes the register software too — Square only processes Square. That coupling is the lock-in, and it is why the fee decision drags the POS decision along with it unless you pick B.

5. What I would do now

  1. Downgrade Square Premium$1,788/yr, independent of every other choice here. Do it regardless of which path you take.
  2. Get a real Helcim quoteTheir 1.93% is a cross-merchant average, not an offer. Ask for pricing on your actual mix: $276k card volume, $26 average ticket, 7,620 transactions.
  3. Price the terminalsSquare hardware will not carry over. This is the unbudgeted number.
  4. Put skateOS + Helcim behind the ONLINE store firstIt has 0 orders today either way — there is no downside. Nobody is standing in line. It proves the payment path with real money and turns that 0 into evidence.
  5. Then decide the register on evidenceParallel run, Square still plugged in, written rollback.
Honest caveat on step 4

Your online volume is small — roughly 27 Square Online orders a quarter. Accumulating 20–30 real transactions will take a couple of months. It is a slow proving ground. But it is a free one, and it is the only place where failure costs you nothing.
