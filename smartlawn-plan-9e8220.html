Executive summary

Smart Lawn NY sells, installs, and services robotic lawn mowers — and its engine is the landscape trade: landscapers buy the robots, deploy them on the properties they already maintain, and Smart Lawn is the sourcing, install, and service layer behind every unit. It is the authorized Segway Navimow and Yarbo dealer and service center for Westchester/Putnam — a two-county exclusive footprint in one of the wealthiest suburban lawn markets in America, at the exact moment Westchester municipalities are restricting gas lawn equipment.

The company is 4 months old as a sales operation and already has real sell-through: 9 units sold to 5 customers ACTUAL, including a commercial fleet account (Lucente Landscaping — one of the largest landscape contractors in lower Westchester) with $15,340 on open invoices ACTUAL. Every sale so far came from zero paid marketing — demos, referrals, and one Facebook listing.

The honest problem, and the reason for this plan: hardware resale alone is a thin-margin treadmill (~18% before manufacturer rebates), and the company is undercapitalized — inventory was floated on distributor credit that is now past due, which blocks restocking exactly when demand is proving out. The opportunity is equally clear: standard pricing carries a 30% margin DOUG'S STANDARD, every unit in the field feeds a $129–$599/month service plan, and one landscaper account buys more robots than ten homeowners — the Lucente account alone is 5 units and $15,340.

The ask: a working-capital partner to (1) clear ~$34,000 of supplier balances, (2) fund a ~$25k inventory + marketing position for the remainder of the 2026 season, in exchange for equity in the Smart Lawn entity when it incorporates (currently a DBA of Second Nature Tree Service LLC, incorporation planned). Structure and percentage to be negotiated — options on page 5.

Why now

  • Regulatory tailwind: Westchester municipalities are phasing in gas-equipment restrictions; battery/robotic is where the market is being pushed.
  • Category inflection: wire-free RTK/vision robots (2024+) removed the boundary-wire installation barrier that killed the last generation of robot mowers.
  • Exclusive-ish position: only authorized Navimow dealer + service center in the two-county footprint, with distributor (CADCO) and manufacturer (Navimow/Segway) relationships already in place.
  • Infrastructure already built and paid for: showroom/shop at Highland Industrial Park, smartlawnny.com with product catalog and lawn-analysis tool, and a full business-management platform (Branch Manager) running quoting, invoicing, payments and automated receipts.

Market

Westchester County alone has ~250,000 households with median incomes near the top of the U.S.; lawn care spend of $2,000–$6,000/season per household is routine ESTIMATE — directionally right, worth a formal TAM pass. For a landscaper, one robot replaces a weekly crew visit on that property all season — they keep charging the client, cut their labor, and mark the hardware up. That's why the sales motion is B2B: a few dozen Westchester landscape contractors control thousands of lawns.

Competition: traditional landscapers (being squeezed on labor + gas rules), big-box retail (sells boxes, no install/service — our warranty-service exclusivity is the moat), and one visible regional competitor (GOAT Robotics, Orange County — Yarbo/Navimow dealer marketing on roadside signs). Nobody in the two-county footprint offers install + service + a landscaper fleet program as a package.

Why Doug wins

  • 15+ years running outdoor-services crews in this exact county (Second Nature Tree, ~$257k gross 2025) — field ops, customer base, and B2B landscaper relationships are already his.
  • Factory relationships in place: dealer training, promo/rebate programs, and NavimowFleet commercial platform access via CADCO/Segway.
  • Software advantage: Branch Manager (built in-house) gives Smart Lawn quoting → invoice → payment → automated receipt flow that competitors run on paper.

Risks — stated plainly

  • Cash discipline: the current supplier arrears happened because inventory was bought ahead of sales. Fix: floor-plan discipline — inventory purchases gated to sell-through, which this plan funds and enforces.
  • Single-supplier exposure: CADCO/Segway relationship is the moat and the choke point. Mitigation: Yarbo second line already held; dealer standing repaired by this raise.
  • Seasonality: mowing is Mar–Oct. Mitigation: Yarbo snow season + winterization plans.
  • Warranty/service load: robots in the field mean service calls. Mitigation: service plans are priced to fund it; Doug's crew already does field service for the tree business.
  • Founder bandwidth: Doug runs two other operations. Mitigation: this raise + the software let Smart Lawn run lean; partner involvement welcome.
