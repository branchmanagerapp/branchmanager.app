TREE GUY SUMMER CAMP™

A modest proposal from the guys who just pulled forty trees off your cabins — and accidentally invented the best vacation in America.

cit·i·ot (noun, plural citiots)

A city person in the woods. Owns a $400 "technical shell." Has never held a technical anything. Calls every tree "an oak." Asks the crew what the chipper retails for, hears the number, and gets real quiet. We love them. They are our most renewable resource.

Dear Postcard Cabins,

In July a storm dropped a small forest on your Western Catskills camp. You called us. We came up with a bucket truck, a chipper, a mini skid steer, and three guys who cannot believe this is a job.

Somewhere around day two — rigging a maple off a cabin roof while the sun came through the hemlocks — we noticed your guests watching us. Not politely. Longingly. These are people who paid real money to sit in a beautiful black box, put their phone in a pouch, and feel something. And there they were at the rope line, watching a chipper eat a brush pile like it was the moon landing. One of them applauded. Quietly. Into his oat-milk latte.

Citiots pay $4,000 for wellness retreats where the main activity is journaling about their father. We are prepared to offer something better: actual trees, actual ropes, actual consequences (small, supervised ones).

The idea

One week a season: Tree Guy Summer Camp. Citiots pay to spend a week "working" alongside a real, insured, professional tree crew on your property. Your hazard trees get handled. Your trails get cleared. Your firewood gets stacked so beautifully it goes on the website. And the citiots go home with calluses, one (1) knot, a trail name they will put in their dating profile, and the best story at every dinner party in Park Slope for the rest of their lives.

Read it again: they pay. To do our job. On your land. Tom Sawyer ran this exact play on a fence in 1876 and it remains undefeated. We are simply Tom Sawyer with a bucket truck and a waiver.

A day at camp

7:00 AMDeli run. Bacon-egg-and-cheese on a roll. A citiot will attempt to order avocado toast. The deli guy will simply stare. This is part of the curriculum.
8:00 AMMorning circle. Stretching, safety talk, helmet fitting, trail-name assignment. You're "Chipmunk." Your buddy is "Mulch." The lawyer is "Liability." The guy who brought his own hatchet from home is "Hatchet Guy" until he dies — that's just how it works. It wasn't a vote. There is no HR. That's the point of the woods.
9:00 AMKnots school. Bowline, clove hitch, running bowline — the same rigging that lowered a maple off your cabin without scratching a gutter. By Friday one citiot will tie a bowline unassisted and cry a little. That's fine. That's normal. That's growth.
11:00 AMClimb time. Harness, doubled rope, closed-course tree, certified climber an arm's length away. Fifteen feet up a hemlock, a man who manages a hedge fund will discover his actual personality.
1:00 PMChipper hour. One citiot at a time, under direct supervision, feeds brush to the machine. We have watched this cure burnout in ninety seconds. Therapists hate this one trick.
3:00 PMFirewood Olympics. Split. Stack. Be judged. The winner gets the good camp chair. The loser gets the folding one that pinches. Stakes are everything.
6:00 PMFirepit. The literal firepit we un-buried this July. S'mores, sore arms, a citiot showing everyone the same knot for the fourth time. Nobody stops him. He's earned this.
