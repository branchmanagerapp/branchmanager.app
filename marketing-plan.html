00 · Read this first

More marketing is not your problem. Thin margin is.

Certain In 2025 you grossed ~$257k and kept ~$12.5k — about 5%. Booking a pile of new residential removals doesn't fix that; if anything, more low-margin work through a fleet that already lost ~16 field-days to breakdowns can dig the hole deeper.

So this plan is deliberately NOT "post 30 times and run Facebook ads." It has one job: use marketing to change the mix and the price — steer toward commercial/recurring and utility line-clearance work, and earn the trust that lets you stop competing on price with the cheapest guy in the parking lot.

If a tactic below doesn't move margin or mix, it's not in here.

01 · Positioning

The insured, credentialed, doesn't-vanish tree company.

Likely Homeowners hiring tree work are scared of two things: property damage and fly-by-night crews. Most of your local competition is a guy with a saw and no insurance. Your edge isn't being cheapest — it's being the one a nervous homeowner (and a property manager who'll get fired for a bad vendor) can trust with a crane near their roof.

Positioning line: Fully insured, credentialed tree care that shows up, does it right, and cleans up — serving Peekskill and Westchester since day one.

Insured & legit

GL + workers comp, DOT, real business. Lead with it — it's the #1 fear-killer and it disqualifies the cheap crews you don't want to compete with anyway.

Credentialed

Likely ISA / TCIA-track credentials matter most to commercial & municipal buyers — the higher-margin ones. Put them where those buyers look.

Local & accountable

Owner-run, Peekskill-rooted, named crew. The company that's still here next season. This is what property managers actually buy.

03 · The growth engine

Reviews are the tree business's flywheel. Build the machine that makes them.

Tree service is a trust-and-fear purchase researched on Google. The company with the most recent, specific 5-star reviews wins the click and holds the higher price. You do dozens of jobs a month and almost certainly ask for a review on approximately none of them. That's the leak.

The review flywheel
  1. Crew finishes a job → before/after photos taken on-site (many already exist in job records).
  2. Same day, an automatic text asks the happy customer for a Google review, with a one-tap link. Likely Branch Manager can fire this off the completed-job event.
  3. Fresh reviews + photos push you up "tree service near me" and let you quote above the cheap crews.
  4. Better-qualified leads who already trust you call → higher close rate, less haggling → more reviews.

Likely This is worth more than any ad budget and costs almost nothing — it's the tree equivalent of the skate park's clip pipeline. Priority #1: wire the post-job review request into Branch Manager.

04 · Content pillars

Four buckets. Proof over promotion.

Before / after job proof

The big removal, the crane job, the clean cleanup. Photos you already take. This is 40% of everything and it sells competence + insurance without saying a price.

Reviews & trust signals

Screenshot real 5-star reviews, "insured & credentialed" reminders, crew in proper gear. Fear-killers on repeat.

Storm & safety

Before a storm: "we're on call." After: fast response photos. Seasonal, high-intent, premium-priced.

The people & the iron

Named crew, the equipment, owner-operated story. Local and accountable — what commercial buyers want to see before they call.

Hard rule: customer-facing marketing (peekskilltree.com, social, ads) never shows rates. Pricing strategy lives internally (§7). Proof and trust sell; numbers only invite price-shopping.

05 · Channels — where to actually spend

Google first. It's where tree jobs are bought.

Google Business Profile

Likely your #1 channel by far. "Tree removal near me" + "emergency tree service" is exactly how these jobs start. Complete profile, photos, and a steady review drip win it. Nearly free.

Local Service Ads / Google Ads

Guessing a modest, measured budget on high-intent storm/removal searches — but ONLY once reviews are flowing, or you're paying to send clicks to a weak profile.

B2B credentials outreach

A one-page insured-&-credentialed capabilities sheet → property managers, HOAs, and Clearway/Lewis sub lists. This is the margin channel; it's email + relationships, not ads.

peekskilltree.com

The trust-closer. Reviews, before/afters, insurance/credentials, service area, easy quote request. No prices. It exists to convert the Google click.

Neighborhood presence

Likely yard signs on visible completed jobs + local FB groups. Cheap, hyper-local, and neighbors literally see the work.

Existing-customer re-marketing

Certain Branch Manager holds your past customers. Seasonal reminders (storm prep, winter pruning) to people who already trust you = the cheapest revenue there is.

07 · Pricing & mix (internal only)

The lever that actually moves the 5%.

Certain Break-even ≈ $14,025/mo; winter nut ≈ $7,500/mo; Dec–Mar runs under break-even. Marketing's job in the numbers:

  • Shift mix toward recurring commercial/PM work to flatten the winter losses (Aug, Dec, Feb, Mar were loss months in 2025-26). Likely the biggest single margin fix.
  • Use trust signals to hold price on residential instead of matching the low bid — reviews let you be the $X, not the $X-minus-20%.
  • Chase line-clearance/ROW where bill rates (market $120–180/hr arborist, crews $225–500+) dwarf residential.
