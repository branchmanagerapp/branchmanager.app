Your 50/50 in the skate park — straight

Where the park stands, how to keep your half protected, and what to get in writing.

Jon — this is laid out from your side, not Doug's. You're already a 50/50 partner in the skate park — 2nd Nature 3, Inc. That's its own company, completely separate from Second Nature Tree LLC (the tree business — that one's Doug's and is not part of this). There's real upside in the park and there's real debt; here's the honest version of both so you're going in with eyes open.

What you're a half-owner of

  • An established, operating skate park at 1 Highland Industrial Park, Peekskill — the pro shop / retail, admissions, lessons, camps, memberships, and the events.
  • A real local following and a brand people know — not starting from zero.
  • A business you and Doug build together, 50/50.

The honest part — the park's debts

The company (2nd Nature 3, Inc.) carries an SBA EIDL loan — its single biggest debt — plus the building lease (the largest monthly cost) and some merchant cash advances / a line of credit. These are the company's obligations: the park's cash flow has to cover them before either owner takes profit.

The exact balances and status matter — get them from the real books (see the checklist below) rather than anyone's memory of the numbers.

Your no-personal-liability position — and how to keep it real

As a 50/50 owner you don't want any of this debt personally on you. "I didn't sign" only protects you if it's actually documented that way.

What keeps you protected

  • Don't personally guarantee any of it — the SBA loan, the lease, or the MCAs/LOC.
  • Get it in writing that the existing debts are the company's (and stay with whoever already signed for them) — not yours.
  • Make the partnership / operating agreement state you carry no personal liability for any pre-existing debt.
  • Have your own attorney review it — not Doug's.

Where you could still be exposed

  • If a lender later asks you to personally guarantee a refinance, that puts it on you. Decide that consciously.
  • As a co-owner, if the company can't pay, creditors can pursue the company's assets — even though they can't come after you personally.
  • Your invested money and sweat are at risk if the turnaround doesn't work — no personal debt, but you can still lose what you put in.

What to lock down in writing (you're already in — so formalize it)

  • A real 50/50 operating / partnership agreement: ownership split, roles, who decides what, and a buy-sell / exit if it ever stops working.
  • Clear statement that the SBA loan + existing debts are the company's (and Doug's, to the extent he signed) — and you carry no personal liability for them.
  • The real books — last 2–3 years of financials, the SBA balance and status, the lease term, and the MCA/LOC balances.
  • How debt service affects distributions — when owners actually get paid, after the debts are covered.
  • A plain list of who has personally guaranteed what.
Bottom line: you're already a half-owner of a real, operating skate park — the upside is real and so is the debt. The smart move now is to get the 50/50 on paper, keep every existing debt off your personal name, and see the real numbers — with your own attorney, not Doug's.

② Internal decision memo

Skate Park Door — let's decide together

How we let paying riders in — let's pick the right way.

John & Nick — here's the whole thing in plain English, my honest recommendation, and the one call we need to make together. There's a clear decision at the bottom.

The 30-second version: we want the door to unlock automatically when someone pays — no staff needed. The hardware to do that (Brivo) is already on the door. The problem isn't the door — it's that we've ended up building the "pay → unlock" logic two different ways, and we need to commit to one. My recommendation: finish it inside SkateOS, tie the unlock to the signed waiver (not just the payment), and hand out a time-limited code instead of a live remote-unlock. That's the version that's safe, reliable, and won't strand a paying rider at a locked door.

Step 1 — How the door actually works

Brivo is the access-control system on the door — the brain behind the lock. Software can tell it things like "let this person in for the next 4 hours" or "open right now." The goal: rider pays → the door opens for them automatically, nobody at the desk.

Step 2 — The real issue: we built it twice

Two separate systems can both talk to the same door. Running both is the thing most likely to break on us.

Two systems = two sets of keys to the same door, and two things to debug when a paying rider is stuck outside at 8pm. The single most important move is to pick one and retire the other.

Step 3 — What I recommend, and why

Finish it in SkateOS — and gate the door on the waiver. Three things make this right instead of just working:

  • Unlock on signed waiver + payment, not payment alone. Opening for someone who hasn't signed our liability release is a real exposure at a skate park.
  • Hand out a time-limited code, don't do a live "remote unlock." A pre-loaded code means a brief internet hiccup won't lock out a paying rider.
  • A manual backup code that always works, plus outage alerts — so if the system goes down, we find out, not the customer.

③ HV Skate · operations

Automating the front door

Run the door off payments and check-ins — open more hours with less front-desk.

Tie the Brivo door access we already have to payments / check-in, so a paying customer or active member gets in automatically. That lets us run unstaffed or extended hours and cut front-desk cost — without changing the systems we already use.

What we already have

  • Brivo — the door-access system on the building. Open, developer-friendly: it can unlock, issue temporary PIN codes, and turn member access on/off via its API.
  • Square — payments / point of sale.
  • SkateOS — sessions, memberships, check-in.

All three are already in place and paid for. The only question is what connects them to the door.

How it would actually work

Day-pass / drop-inCustomer pays (Square / SkateOS)→System issues a time-limited PIN→Door unlocks for their session
MembersMembership active in SkateOS/Square→Their Brivo badge/app turns on automatically→They let themselves in; lapsed = auto shut off

No one has to be at the desk to let people in or check whether they paid — the payment is the key.
