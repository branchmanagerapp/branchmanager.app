1. What this is

Northeast Intrepid = becoming the regional dealer / demo center for Intrepid compact loaders (Belgian Knikmops — KM85 / KM100 / KM130 / KM180 / KM250 TELE) plus the Jobeau stump-grinder and chipper line, for the tri-state corridor (Hudson Valley / Westchester / Putnam / Fairfield). The importer has sold ~150 units over 7–8 years nationally with no Northeast showroom, demo, or service presence. That gap is the whole opportunity — and service is the piece that's been missing.

3. Does it really make sense? (the honest read)

At the base case, ~$49,500 of gross machine margin is side-business money, not a salary — and that's before my carrying costs. So on machine sales alone, this is not something that replaces a paycheck in year one. Here's why I still think it pencils:

  • It rides on top of what I already run. The tree business, the demo yard, and Branch Manager (my back office) are already here. This is an added channel, not a company built from zero.
  • The overhead is deliberately tiny. Start outdoors on the demo yard (no showroom rent year one), service through independent partners (you and Ron), inventory on consignment so I can return what doesn't sell. A slow season can't bury me — that's built into the structure on purpose.
  • The margin stack is the real story. 15% on the machine is the floor. Attachments, Jobeau grinders/chippers, rentals, parts, and used trade-ins all layer higher-margin revenue on the same customer and the same demo.
  • I'm building an asset, not just chasing a sale. Every machine sold is a customer, a territory foothold, and — this is your part — a service relationship that recurs for years.

The honest risk: high-ticket, low-volume iron. Sell three in a slow year and the machine margin is modest. The consignment + floor-plan structure is exactly what keeps that from being fatal — I'm not floating a yard full of machines I owe on.
