The plan

How we'd work together

This is the part that matters most. Everything here is a starting point for us to change together.

1. One company, six equal owners

We form one LLC for the yard. Each of us owns an equal share and has an equal vote. A short written operating agreement covers everything below, so nobody is guessing later.

2. Everyone chips in the same each month

Each owner pays an equal share of the yard's fixed costs: rent ($583 each), plus insurance, power and the office trailer, split six ways. The dues are the same whether it's a busy month or a slow one.

3. Wood in gets credited to you

You drop logs from your jobs at the yard, and every load is logged to your name. When that wood gets sold, you get paid for it at a set rate per load. Bring more wood and you earn more.

4. The yard does the processing

One shared processor and splitter, not six. Wood gets cut, split and stacked to season at the yard. One of us, or a hired yard manager, runs it day to day and gets paid for that time. Anyone whose crew works a yard day gets paid for the labor.

5. One name sells it

The yard sells firewood under its own brand, with one phone number, one website and one price list that the yard sets. Your customers who want wood get sent to the yard. Deliveries go out on our trucks, and the truck owner gets paid per delivery.

6. How the money comes back out

Every month, in this order:

  • Pay the yard's bills
  • Pay each owner for the wood they brought in, plus any labor and deliveries
  • Whatever's left is profit, split equally among the six owners

Everyone can see the same books at any time: wood in, sales, bills and payouts.

7. Joining and leaving

Anyone can leave with notice, and the others buy back their share at a fair, agreed price. A new person joins only if the owners vote them in.

8. What stays separate

Your tree business stays 100% yours: your customers, your crews, your prices. The yard is only about firewood.

Why it's better than going it alone

  • A real yard with seasoned wood and delivery earns a better price than scattered sellers can get.
  • Logs off your jobs turn into money instead of a disposal cost.
  • Winter income for everyone when tree work slows down.
