How Payroll & Job Costing Work

Second Nature Tree · Branch Manager · last updated 21 Aug 2026
Why this page exists. On 20 Aug 2026 the automatic payroll writer put 36 rows into the books from GPS alone. Three were wrong and Doug caught them by eye: six hours paid to two people for a Sunday he spent in Hampton Bays; David credited 0.75 h for a full day; Doug credited 4.25 h for a twelve-hour Tuesday. Every one traced to the same design flaw — hours were attached to trucks, not people, so a dead tracker silently erases someone's workday.

1 · Each person's hours are measured differently

David — job-site arrival → job-site departure. He meets the crew at the job, so his clock is the site, never the yard.
Catherine — her truck out of the yard → back at the yard.
Doug — the Ram arriving at the yard → the Ram leaving for home. Commute is excluded on both ends.

These are not interchangeable. Deriving David's hours from a truck's yard times was wrong even on days the tracker worked — it just didn't show until the dongle failed.

2 · What counts as a work day

A site counts toward a job only with 60+ minutes on site. Below that it is treated as an estimate or a drive-by.

Doug, on 8 Aug at 4 Terrace Drive: “we were just looking at the tree because it was down the road from another job.” That visit spanned 50 minutes. The three real work days spanned 436, 492 and 366 minutes.

3 · Revenue splits across the days actually worked

A multi-day job divides evenly. The number of days is derived from GPS presence, not from the job record — jobs carry one date, but a job can run three days.

$7,500 job ÷ 3 days on site = $2,500 per day

The whole week's stops are clustered into sites first, and each site is geocoded and matched to a job once. An earlier version re-derived an address every day, so one job read as “2 Terrace Drive” Monday, “4 Orchard Drive” Tuesday and “4 Terrace Drive” Wednesday — a 40 m difference in parking broke the match.

7 · Known limits — read before trusting a number

  • The Ram's dongle is failing. On 17 Aug it logged 241 pings all in motion then died 7 km from home. Silent 18 and 20 Aug. Every hour of David's depends on it.
  • Bouncie reports while moving. It sleeps when parked, so idle and PTO time is largely invisible — no gear, no engine hours, no odometer.
  • A manual edit is only safe if it locks. The cron rewrites the last 4 days every 2 hours; edits now set locked, which that function skips.
  • Job / Revenue / Expenses / P&L rows are desktop-only — they do not render on a phone yet.
  • Payroll hours are the unverified auto rows until each is checked against texts.
