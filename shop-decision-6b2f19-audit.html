Is skateOS ready?

A straight answer on whether skateOS can run the shop, based on what is deployed and what the database shows.
OverviewPricingMigrationArchitectureskateOS audit
Correction — my first read of this was wrong

I audited skateos.com and concluded there was no POS, because /admin, /pos and /checkout all return 404. That was an incomplete audit. The application is on a different subdomain: app.skateos.com — a 2.9 MB single-page app.

skateOS is far more built than I first said. The findings below replace that earlier read.

Verdict

Not ready — but not because it is unbuilt. Because it is unproven.

skateOS has more skatepark-specific capability than Square will ever have. What it does not have is evidence that it works under load. Your park ran 10,694 payments and $328,025 through Square last year. In the same database, skateOS shows 0 orders, 0 gift cards and 3 check-ins. It is a finished-looking system that has never carried a real day.

This is the part worth saying plainly

Door access, waivers, lessons, camps, bus manifests, progress reports, member cards — Square does not do any of that and never will. On skatepark operations, skateOS is not behind Square. It is well ahead of it.

Security — fix this whether or not you ever migrate

The Supabase anon key is embedded in the public shop page. With it I confirmed 27,639 customer records and 10,751 sales records return HTTP 200. Row-level security is not restricting them.

That is not a migration issue. That is live, today, on a key anyone can copy out of your page source in ten seconds. It outranks every other item here.

What a real migration would take

  1. Lock down RLSNon-negotiable and independent of everything else. 27,639 customer records are readable right now.
  2. Open and underwrite a Helcim merchant accountApplication, underwriting, approval. Then compare its real rate against the 2.40% + 15¢ you already have — do not assume it wins.
  3. Buy and test card terminalsSquare hardware will not carry over.
  4. Reconcile the catalogue1,409 rows against 1,102 Square items with 30% overlap. Decide what is real, once.
  5. Prove offline behaviourPull the Wi-Fi mid-sale, repeatedly, until you trust it. This is the test that actually predicts a bad Saturday.
  6. Parallel run for 30–60 daysskateOS ringing alongside Square, reconciled nightly. Do not cut over on faith.
  7. Train all seven staffWith Square still on the counter as the fallback.
  8. Cut over with a written rollbackSquare stays plugged in for a full season.

Steps 2 and 3 carry real cost I have not verified — Helcim rates and terminal pricing need quoting before this becomes a budget rather than a plan.

What I would actually do

Do not migrate the POS. You already said you cannot trust an untested register in front of a crowd, and the data agrees with you — 0 orders is not a system anyone should put in front of a Saturday line.

But do not shelve skateOS either. Its real value was never as a Square replacement. Run it as the park-operations layer — door access, waivers, lessons, camps, check-ins, member cards, staff clock-in — all things Square genuinely cannot do, and where the lessons table already proves it works. Leave the money on Square, where 10,694 payments a year say it belongs.

That splits the risk correctly: the part that is proven keeps taking money, and the part that is genuinely better gets used for what it is better at.
