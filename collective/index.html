How it works

Four things a cooperative gives every member — that none of them could afford alone.

💸

Share the costs — legitimately

One entity carries the big stuff and splits it by a clear formula: group liability & auto insurance, shared chippers/cranes/bucket trucks, and a real back office (books, payroll, sales tax, licensing, certs). Properly on the books and deductible — not a handshake.

🛡️

Be legit & covered

Everyone's a real W-2 owner-employee with real workers comp and commercial coverage. That's not just protection — it's the certificate of insurance that unlocks utility, municipal, and high-value insured work you can't bid solo.

🏥

Big-company benefits

Pooled, the group affords what no solo guy can: group health, a retirement plan, paid time off, safety & climbing training, equipment financing at company rates, and bulk pricing on chain, fuel, parts & PPE.

📈

Real equity

Members buy in once and own a piece — one member, one vote. Profit is shared by the work you put in (patronage), and your ownership builds in a capital account that pays out when you retire or move on. Your sweat finally becomes equity.

The structure, plain

  1. Form one cooperative entityIndependent operators join a single worker-owned company instead of staying separate LLCs.
  2. Members buy in & get a voteA modest membership share makes you an owner — one member, one vote, democratic say in how it's run.
  3. Costs pool centrallyInsurance, equipment, software, and the back office are carried by the co-op and shared by a set formula.
  4. Profit shared by patronageAt year end, surplus is paid out based on the work each member contributed — not just who put in cash.
  5. Equity builds & pays outEach member's ownership accrues in a capital account, redeemable when they leave or retire.

Set up with a cooperative attorney and a CPA — exact entity, buy-in, and patronage rules vary by state. This is the model, not legal or tax advice.
