Standard rates & accountability

Pay that's fair, predictable, and based on what the trade actually pays — plus a straight rule for when money's tight.

Nobody negotiates pay by who they're friends with. We use standard industry rates for every role, the co-op adopts them, and they only change by group vote.

📊 Every figure below is an industry-standard reference (typical U.S. / Northeast market) — adjust for experience & region. Not legal or financial advice.

Founding members (owner-operators)

Founding members are paid the going rate for the role they fill on a job — the same scale above. Ownership doesn't buy a fatter hourly. Your real reward as an owner comes through year-end patronage and equity, not a higher wage. Everyone works at the rate.

Sales & estimating

Industry-standard sales commission: ~8–12% of the sold job (use 10% as the default), paid on jobs that close and collect.

Rewards bringing in and closing work — without padding prices. Members who sell their own work can take it as commission or fold it into patronage.

Customer billing / the price floor

Crews typically bill ~$1,500–$3,000+ per day depending on equipment and job type. The co-op sets a per-job price floor every bid must clear — see the rate calculator on the How it works page. No lowballing below the floor.

⚠️ How we handle losing money

Slow seasons and bad jobs happen. Here's the order it's handled in.

1. Reserve first. The co-op keeps a cash reserve (a set % of revenue). Normal slow patches and small losses come out of it — not out of pockets.
2. Share the loss. If a year ends in the red beyond the reserve, the shortfall is split among members by contribution and reduces each member's capital (equity) account.
3. Capital call if needed. If cash is genuinely needed to keep running, members may be asked to contribute their share to keep the co-op solvent.

Pay your share — or lose equity, then you're out

Ownership is real: real upside, real responsibility. The rule is simple and it protects the people pulling their weight.

  1. Every member owes their share — buy-in installments, their portion of a loss or capital call, or what they owe for negligent damage.
  2. Don't pay it → it comes out of your equity (your capital account is debited what you owe).
  3. Equity runs out → you're out. If your capital account hits zero and you still owe or won't contribute, you forfeit membership.
  4. Fair process: written notice + a set window to make it right before any forfeiture, decided by group vote. No surprises, but no free riders either.

The point: nobody carries a member who won't carry their share. Pull your weight and your equity grows; don't, and it drains until there's nothing left to stand on.
