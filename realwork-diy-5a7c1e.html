RealWork Labs: use it, or build it?

Eli's pitch from the Sept 11 call, taken apart. What the product actually does, what you already own, and the part worth copying.

Verdict: don't buy it. Build the one piece that matters. RealWork's whole value is a public map of your jobs and reviews by neighborhood, pushed to Google. Branch Manager already GPS-stamps every job photo, geocodes every client, and has Google-review tools. The missing piece is publishing that to peekskilltree.com and Google Business Profile. That is a weekend of work, not a subscription. Monday's 4pm demo is optional; skip it if you're on a job.

What RealWork actually is

A field-service add-on that gets crews to check in at each job (photo, GPS, a few words), asks the customer for a review tied to that check-in, then plots all of it on a "review map" widget on your website and posts it to your Google Business Profile. Their claim is local SEO: prospects see you worked on their street, and Google Maps ranks you for "tree service near me" in those neighborhoods.

  • Integrates with Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, JobNimbus to automate the review request after a job.
  • Per-job "stories" (photos + text) that live on your site as indexed pages.
  • AI writes the job descriptions and review replies.
  • Pricing is not published. Every quote comes from a sales call; G2 and the comparison sites say the same. Reviews are mixed: portfolio value is real, onboarding complaints are common.

Sources: realworklabs.com (vendor, read Sept 13 2026) · G2 reviews · Review Wheel comparison (a competitor, 2026) · Local Search Forum thread. Eli's email Sept 11, 12:01pm.

The DIY version, in three steps

1. Publish a "Recent work near you" map on peekskilltree.comOne page. Pulls the last ~100 completed jobs from Branch Manager (town-level pin, one photo, service type, month). Rounded to the street so no customer address is exposed. Leaflet + OpenStreetMap tiles, same stack the estimates map already uses. Effort: one build session.
2. Turn completed jobs into indexed job pagesWhen a job is marked complete with photos, generate a public page: "Oak removal in Cortlandt Manor, Sept 2026" with 2–3 photos, a two-line description, the town page link, and Review + Service schema. Auto-linked from the map and the matching town page. This is the local-SEO engine RealWork sells. Effort: one build session, then it runs itself.
3. Tag reviews to jobs and push posts to GoogleWhen a Google review lands, match it to the job by customer name and pin it on the map. Feed the same completed-job card into SocialBranch as a draft Google Business Profile post. You approve, it posts. Effort: half a session; the posting side already exists.

What to keep from Eli's pitch

  • The idea is right: proximity proof sells tree work. Neighbors trust the crew that did the house next door.
  • The discipline is the hard part, not the software: crews take the photos at every job (Branch Cam already makes that the default), and someone approves the post.
  • Nothing else. The integrations, the widget, the AI copy are all things Branch Manager either has or can have for free.

Rules that stay

  • No prices on anything public. Job pages show service and town, never the invoice.
  • Nothing auto-sends to a customer. Review requests stay in the approve queue.
  • Street-level only on the public map. No house numbers, no names without written consent.
