Inventory ramp — the floor fleet what Chris wants to see

Honest about where we start — and a clear path to carrying real inventory by year 3, which is what a manufacturer wants from a dealer.

YearOn the floor
1My KM100 (bought) + Todd's KM130 for demos — two machines, our own iron, no capital ask of you.
2Add a KM85 (the small end) and a second KM130 that's ours to sell — start rounding out the range.
3Real held floor: add the big KM180 & KM250, plus more KM100 / KM130 stock — a machine in every size on the ground.
4–5Deeper stock on the fast-movers (KM100 / KM130), replenished out of sales.

How it's funded: the first sale's profit buys in the next floor model, then half the profit of every sale after, until the fleet is bought in. The floor bootstraps out of sales, not capital up front — so by year 3 you're not looking at a consignment-only dealer, you're looking at one carrying its own inventory across the whole lineup.

Parts department — the recurring line

The engines are Kubota (D902 / D1105 / V1505) — the most common small-diesel family there is, so parts are cheap, available, and never a special order. Parts is the recurring, high-margin piece that builds as machines hit the field.

  • ~$200–400 in parts per machine per year est once it's in service (filters, belts, glow plugs, wear items).
  • ~30–40% gross margin on parts (industry norm) — small fast-movers carry more, big-ticket less.
  • Shipping is small and usually passed to the customer · inventory is up-front stock (working capital, not a yearly cost) · warehousing is FREE — it runs off the Second Nature Skatepark shipping bench.

Base assumptions — why this is hard to break

This plan doesn't bet on Intrepid getting more popular — only on it continuing to sell as well as it does right now. Intrepid has placed ~150 machines over 7–8 years at 20–30 a year nationally — almost entirely on word of mouth, with no demo center, no demo program, and no Northeast presence at all. All this plan needs is for me to capture a small, conservative slice of that existing demand — and it starts at just a quarter of the regional potential.

So as long as Intrepid keeps selling as well as — or better than — it does today, this plan holds. Adding a real demo center, local service, and two shows a year should make the machine sell better here, not worse — so the honest expectation is upside on these numbers, not downside. The only real variables are the editable cost lines (rent, insurance, booth), and those are set conservative on purpose.

On the 30% / $18k per machine: that's a gross markup, realistic for an exclusive niche import — you buy ~25–30% off MSRP and sell near it, because the brand isn't price-shopped across a dozen dealers like a Deere or a Bobcat. (A franchised mass-market dealer makes only ~7% on a new machine and lives on parts & service.) It's gross per unit, before freight, demo wear, and any financing buy-down — real net lands below the sticker, which the expense lines already pull down to it.
