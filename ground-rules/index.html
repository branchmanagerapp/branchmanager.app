DRAFT · FOR DISCUSSION
Before money's involved

How we run this — and the traps we avoid

Friends + business goes bad when the rules are assumed instead of agreed. Here's everything we settle up front, in plain language. Nothing's final — this is the conversation.

A co-op among people who trust each other is the strongest version of this — and the easiest to wreck if we stay vague. The fix isn't distrust. It's writing down what we'd all assume anyway, so nobody's guessing and no resentment builds. Read it, mark what you disagree with, and we hash it out together.

⚠️ The pitfalls that sink groups like ours

Name them now so they don't surprise us later.

The free-rider. One guy hustles, another coasts — but profit's shared. Kills morale fastest of all. Fixed by paying for work done + sharing profit by contribution, not equally-no-matter-what.
Side-work leakage. Members running cash jobs on the side using co-op leads, gear, insurance, or name. The #1 trust-killer. (Its own section below.)
Equipment abuse & repair bills. Shared gear gets run hard and nobody owns the upkeep — or one careless guy's breakdown drains the whole group's wallet. Needs a maintenance reserve plus a clear wear-vs-negligence rule.
Lowballing. One member underbids to win work and quietly drags down everyone's rates and margins. Bids have to meet a price floor.
Cherry-picking. Everyone wants the big removals; nobody wants the small, far, messy jobs. Work has to be shared fairly — the good and the ugly.
Unequal buy-in. One brings a $150k bucket truck, another a chainsaw. If that's not valued fairly, the big contributor feels robbed.
Whose clients are they? If someone leaves, do they take "their" book? Decide before, not during a breakup.
One bad job hits everyone. A member's accident or sloppy work spends the group's insurance and reputation. Shared coverage = shared standards.
Cash discipline. Everyone drawing too much, nothing left for slow months or a blown hydraulic line.
Deadlock & egos. Equal votes + strong personalities = stalemate. Need a tiebreak and a clear day-to-day decision-maker.
Off-the-books cash. One member hiding revenue poisons the whole entity's legitimacy and taxes. All work, on the books, no exceptions.
"We're buddies, we'll figure it out." The most expensive sentence in small business. We figure it out now, in writing.

📋 The rules we need to agree on

Each one's a decision. Recommended starting point in green — push back on any of it.

🔑 Side work — the big one

The fastest way to break trust is unclear rules on outside jobs. Three tiers makes it simple:

All co-op
Tree work goes through the co-op. Any tree/removal/pruning job — booked, billed, insured, and shared through the co-op. That's the deal.
Banned
No side jobs using co-op anything. No using the group's leads, clients, equipment, insurance, or name for personal cash work. No quoting a co-op lead "on the side." This is the bright line.
Carve-out
Disclosed exceptions only. Pre-existing personal clients can be grandfathered if listed up front; favors for family / out-of-trade work (e.g., a landscaping job) okay with disclosure. When in doubt, bring it to the group.
Why it matters: we all carry the same insurance and reputation. A side job done under our umbrella but off our books is everyone's liability with none of the shared upside. Clear tiers protect the guys doing it right.

🔧 Equipment — maintenance & "someone's stupidity"

Besides side work, this is what breeds resentment fastest: who keeps the gear running, and who pays when it breaks.

Co-op pays
Routine upkeep & honest wear. Scheduled maintenance and normal mechanical failure come from a maintenance reserve (a set % of revenue kept aside). Every machine has a steward and a preventive-maintenance schedule tracked in BranchManager.
You care for it
Daily care is on the operator. Fluids, chains, greasing, walk-arounds — logged. You run it that day, you look after it that day.
You pay
Negligence isn't wear. Damage from carelessness, skipped maintenance, misuse, or running gear you're not trained on is on the responsible member (or a set deductible). Quick written incident report every time; repeat offenders lose equipment privileges.
Why it matters: shared equipment only works if everyone treats it like their own. Reward the careful — don't let one guy's stupidity drain everyone's reserve.

🎯 How jobs get bid — and the price floor

One member lowballing to win work drags down everyone's rates and margins. Bids have to meet a standard.

Start: every job must clear a minimum price floor (built from the day-rate math — see the working-days calculator on the "How it works" page) and a target margin before it's booked. One shared estimating guide / price list so quotes are consistent member to member. Big or oddball jobs get a second set of eyes before they go out. BranchManager can flag any quote that falls below the co-op floor. No race to the bottom.

Who's a member, and what's the buy-in?

Ownership has to mean something to be worth protecting.

Start: equal membership buy-in (cash and/or valued equipment), one member = one vote regardless of size. New members admitted by group vote, with a probation period before full ownership vests.

Hiring regular (non-owner) employees

The co-op will need crew who aren't owners — groundsmen, climbers, office help.

Start: the co-op hires W-2 employees at fair wages with benefits, covered under the group insurance & workers comp. They earn a paycheck — not ownership or patronage — but a clear path to membership after a vesting period rewards the keepers. Day-to-day hiring within budget is handled by the ops lead; big additions go to a group vote.

How does everyone get paid?

Cash flow fairness now + ownership reward later.

Start: fair day-rate / wage (W-2) for work performed — you're paid for what you do — plus year-end profit share (patronage) by contribution, after the co-op keeps a reserve. Steady pay + real upside.

Rates are set — nobody gives themselves a raise

Worker-owners still have to work at an agreed rate, or the whole thing wobbles.

Start: pay rates are set by the group and changed only by vote — and only when the numbers actually support it. You don't bump your own pay, and you work at the agreed rate. Extra reward shows up as year-end patronage, not a quiet raise. Owning it means living by the rate, not above it.

Sharing the good jobs and the ugly ones

If everyone cherry-picks the gravy work, the small/far/messy jobs never get done.

Start: work is distributed fairly — everyone takes their share of the less-glamorous jobs (small cleanups, long drives, storm grunt work), not just the big removals. The hub schedule balances it; turning down reasonable assigned work affects your standing & patronage.

What's expected of each member — and what if someone doesn't pull weight?

The free-rider problem, handled before it festers.

Start: a minimum availability/commitment everyone agrees to; work tracked per member in BranchManager (jobs, hours, revenue) so patronage is based on real numbers, not vibes. Persistent underperformance → reviewed by the group.

Who owns the equipment, and how is shared gear handled?

The $150k-truck-vs-chainsaw problem.

Start: equipment brought in is valued and credited to that member (buy-in or lease-back to the co-op); shared gear is co-op-owned, booked through BranchManager, with a maintenance + damage policy.

Whose clients and leads are they?

Prevents the ugly breakup fight.

Start: all leads & clients booked through the co-op belong to the co-op; pre-existing personal clients are listed at the start. Leads distributed fairly (by area/availability). Non-solicit of co-op clients if someone leaves.

How do we make decisions — and break ties?

No deadlock, no power grab.

Start: day-to-day handled by a rotating or elected operations lead; big calls (new members, big purchases, removing a member) need a supermajority vote. Written dispute-resolution step before anyone walks.

Insurance, safety & standards

Shared coverage only works with shared standards.

Start: everyone meets agreed safety/quality standards & certs; group carries GL, auto & workers comp; written accident protocol. A member repeatedly creating claims is a group matter.

Books & transparency

Legitimacy is the whole point — it can't be optional.

Start: all revenue on the books, no off-book cash, ever. Books open to all members in BranchManager. One bookkeeping standard, one CPA.

What happens when someone leaves, retires, or can't work?

The exit is where unwritten deals explode.

Start: a written buyout formula for a member's capital account (paid over time so it doesn't sink cash flow); non-solicit on the way out; clear rules for death/disability. Everybody knows the door before they need it.

Nothing here is final.

This is the starting draft so we walk into the founding talk with the same picture. We decide it together — then a co-op attorney + CPA put the agreed version into the operating agreement and bylaws.

Discussion draft only — not legal or tax advice. Exact structure (entity, buy-in, patronage, buyout) must be set with a cooperative attorney and CPA.
