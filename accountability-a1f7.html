✅ CLOSED Aug 2
Fixed, deployed, and live-verified with a real attack probe: a tampered $0.50 request against a $100 canary invoice was floored to the full $100.00 server-side. Smoke test green.

1. A customer could underpay an invoice and it still marked PAID IN FULL — FIXED

The online pay link lets the amount be lowered (down to $0.50), and the charge amount isn't re-checked on the server. When payment succeeds, the invoice is flipped to paid / balance $0 regardless of how much was actually paid. So someone could owe $5,000, pay $0.50, and get a "paid in full" receipt.

Costs you: real revenue, silently. No evidence anyone's done it — but the hole is open.
Fix: compute the charge server-side from the actual invoice balance (your portal-pay-all function already does this correctly — copy it), drop the adjustable amount on the pay link, and only mark "paid" when the amount received covers the balance (otherwise record a partial payment).
stripe-webhook, stripe-charge, stripe-create-link

2. Invoice/quote numbers can silently strand a record on one phone

New invoice/quote numbers are picked from what's on that device only. The database enforces "no duplicate numbers," so if two devices pick the same number (or bulk-generation reuses one), the second save is rejected and retries forever — the record lives on that one phone and never reaches the cloud or your books. Bulk invoice generation is especially exposed because it counts rows instead of using the real highest number.

Costs you: an invoice you think exists, missing from every other device and your totals.
Fix: hand out numbers atomically from the database (the mechanism was written for jobs but never wired in; extend it to invoices/quotes), and fix bulk-generate to use the real max.
db.js nextInvNum/nextQuoteNum · invoices.js _doGenerate
