Correction to what I told you earlier

I said Supabase was a synced copy of Square. That is not true. I compared them directly: of 400 active Supabase products, only 121 (30.2%) exist in your Square catalog by name. Supabase holds things Square does not (Orbit gum, Red Bull, youth sweatshirts) and Square holds roughly a thousand items the website has never seen.

The local script I assumed was the sync — square_sync.py — runs the other direction: it pushes invoice line items into Square. There is no Square → website sync at all. These are two unrelated product databases that happen to share a third of their names.

Security — found, then fixed

resolved 1 Sept 2026

My first read said three tables. A full sweep found twelve — every one returning real rows to the anon key in your public shop page:

TableRows exposed
customers27,639
sales10,751
products1,409
lessons141
categories74
checkins3
subscriptions2
orders, order_items, invoices, gift_cards, form_submissions0 today — open, and exposed the moment they fill

This is the same failure class as the Branch Manager RLS breach in my notes. The key being public is normal and fine — that is what an anon key is for. What is not fine is that it can read customer and sales tables. The fix is RLS policies on the database, not hiding the key.

Writes were open too — customers, sales and products all accepted a write probe rather than refusing it.

Closed, and verified from the outside

Nine tables now return nothing to the public key. products (1,409) and categories (74) still serve the storefront by design. Public INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE all return 401 permission denied. The reservation form still submits. The shop page renders at 375px with zero console errors.

The first attempt silently failed, and the lesson is worth keeping: RLS policies are permissive-OR. Enabling RLS and adding policies does not close a table while a pre-existing open policy is still there. Existing policies must be dropped first — and a security migration should end with a query that reports its own final state. “Success. No rows returned” was not evidence of anything.

What this means for the migration question

The 30% overlap changes the answer materially. There is no sync to repair — there is no sync. Pointing the site at Square is not a fix to a broken pipe, it is replacing a duplicate product database with the real one, and it makes the roughly 1,000 Square-only items sellable online for the first time.

The reverse also becomes a question you have to answer: the Supabase-only items — gum, soda, youth sweatshirts — either get created in Square or they stop appearing on the site. I would not guess which. That is a real inventory decision, not a technical one.
