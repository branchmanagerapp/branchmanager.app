1 · The thesis in one paragraph

Soft washing is not pressure washing. You apply a dilute sodium-hypochlorite (SH) mix at low pressure, the biological growth dies and rinses, and the surface is never blasted. That matters because it's the only method that's safe on slate, and slate is where the money is. The exact same hour of labor is worth 4–8× more on a slate roof than on an asphalt one — you use less chemical and charge more.

You are not selling a wash. You are selling the roofer a lead-generation and deferral tool. That's why this sells so much easier B2B than door-to-door.

5 · Risks — the ones that actually bite

  1. Insurance exclusion. Most tree-service GL policies exclude pressure/soft washing. Working uninsured on a $1.4M roof is the single way this goes catastrophically wrong. Confirm coverage before job one.
  2. Chemical damage claims. SH kills plants, stains, and corrodes metal flashing/gutters if you're careless. Pre-wet and rinse landscaping, tarp what matters, and photograph everything before and after — same discipline as your tree photo documentation.
  3. Height + wet slate. Slate is lethally slick wet. Bucket-truck access (which you have) rather than walking the roof is both the safe answer and the sales answer.
  4. Flashing and existing damage. Brink flagged flashings specifically. Document pre-existing conditions or you'll own every leak the homeowner notices next rain.
  5. It's another thing to store and maintain. Brink's own counsel on the FEMA side applies here — don't buy the Tier-2 rig until repeat work is real.
