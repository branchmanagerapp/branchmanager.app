1 · Schema (lean — 2 new tables, 2 new columns, reuse the rest)

-- Immutable snapshot taken when a job closes. THE record of truth for that payout.
CREATE TABLE job_costings (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  tenant_id UUID NOT NULL,
  job_id UUID REFERENCES jobs(id) UNIQUE,     -- one costing per job
  closed_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT now(),
  revenue NUMERIC(10,2),                      -- job total excl. sales tax
  materials_cost NUMERIC(10,2),               -- real recorded job costs (COGS)
  labor_cost NUMERIC(10,2),                   -- Σ hours × role rate (owner included)
  labor_burden NUMERIC(10,2),                 -- labor × burden% (payroll tax + WC)
  overhead_rate NUMERIC(5,4),                 -- trailing-12 rate AT close (frozen)
  overhead_cost NUMERIC(10,2),                -- revenue × overhead_rate
  margin NUMERIC(10,2),                       -- revenue − mat − labor − burden − OH
  pool_pct NUMERIC(4,3),                      -- setting at close (frozen)
  pool_amount NUMERIC(10,2),                  -- pool_pct × max(margin, 0)
  snapshot JSONB,                             -- full math trail shown on receipts
  status TEXT DEFAULT 'pending'               -- pending → approved → paid
    CHECK (status IN ('pending','approved','paid','void'))
);

-- One row per crew member per closed job = their cut + their receipt.
CREATE TABLE profit_share_allocations (
  id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  tenant_id UUID NOT NULL,
  job_costing_id UUID REFERENCES job_costings(id),
  team_member_id UUID REFERENCES team_members(id),
  member_name TEXT,
  hours NUMERIC(5,2),
  labor_paid NUMERIC(10,2),                   -- their fixed-rate wage on this job
  tier SMALLINT,                              -- 1/2/3 at time of close (frozen)
  tier_multiplier NUMERIC(4,2),
  weight NUMERIC(10,4),                       -- labor_paid × tier_multiplier
  share_amount NUMERIC(10,2),                 -- pool × weight / Σweights
  receipt_token TEXT UNIQUE,                  -- public receipt URL (like pay.html)
  status TEXT DEFAULT 'pending'
    CHECK (status IN ('pending','approved','paid','void')),
  paid_at TIMESTAMPTZ, payment_method TEXT
);

2 · Overhead — derived from your real transactions, not assumed

Formula: at each job close, overhead rate = trailing-12-month overhead spend ÷ trailing-12-month net revenue, computed live from bank_transactions and frozen into the costing snapshot. It self-updates monthly as real spending changes — nobody ever picks a number.

What counts as overhead (account codes): all operating spend except what the job already pays directly — direct COGS (51–54xx: subs, materials, disposal — charged per-job), wages 6100 (charged per-job at fixed rates), payroll tax 6110 + workers comp 6310 (applied as a labor-burden % on the labor line), and pass-through sales-tax remittance 6130/6135 (excluded from both sides). Owner draws (7000) and transfers (71–73xx) are never costs.

3 · The margin math at job close

margin  = revenue − materials − (labor × (1 + 3.1% burden)) − (revenue × overhead%)
pool    = pool% × max(margin, 0)        -- never negative, never clawed back
share_i = pool × (labor_paid_i × tier_mult_i) ÷ Σ all weights

Labor rule (locked, per your spec): everyone on the job — including you when you're climbing — is a fixed line at their role rate. Salespeople quote against known labor costs. Profit-share is a layer on top; the labor rate never wiggles.

Worked example EXAMPLE — not a real payout — $4,000 job (excl. tax), $300 dump fee, Ryan 8h + David 8h + Doug 8h @ $30, overhead mode A (74%), pool 30%:

Revenue$4,000.00
Materials/disposal− $300.00
Labor 24h × $30 × 1.031− $742.32
Overhead 74%− $2,960.00
Job margin$0 → no pool

FLAG — this is the uncomfortable truth, and it's the point. At today's real overhead, an average-priced job produces no pool. The pool appears exactly where the business actually makes money: storm work, big-ticket removals, jobs that beat the average revenue-per-day. Same job at $6,500 → margin ≈ $647 → pool ≈ $194. The dashboard makes that visible to the crew — which is the open-book philosophy doing its job: everyone sees that "work faster, quote better, spend less on trucks" literally creates the pool. It also means this system gives you a crew-aligned reason to fix overhead, not just a payout gadget.

  • Skill sets the floor, tier adds loyalty: the role rate (climber > ground > new guy) is the wage floor; the tier multiplier only scales their slice of the pool weight (labor_paid × multiplier).
  • Ownership income stays separate (locked, per your spec): your return as owner is a company-level admin expense line in the books — it never appears inside job labor or the pool math. When you work a job you're paid the role rate like anyone else.

5 · Where crew attribution comes from (the one field-workflow change)

Today no completed job knows who worked it. Fix: the existing crew clock-in gets a job picker (time_entries.job_id already exists, just unused), and at job close the costing pulls crew + hours from time entries on that job. Fallback: the close screen shows a pre-checked crew list to confirm — 5 seconds on a phone. No new data entry, just linking what's already tracked.

6 · Payout delivery + receipt

  1. Job marked complete → costing computed → payout queue on your dashboard (same pattern as the draft-message queue: nothing pays out without your tap).
  2. You approve → allocations post to earnings_ledger → you send the money same-day (Zelle/ACH as you do now — BM records it; BM does not move money in v1).
  3. Each member gets a customer-style receipt — token URL, same template infrastructure as payment receipts: job total, materials, labor, overhead %, margin, pool, their share, tier badge, YTD totals. Full math, nothing hidden.
