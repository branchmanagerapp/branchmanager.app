🧹 Shop & Fridge — Clean & Stocked

A simple system for keeping the shop, break area, and fridge handled — one that actually sticks with a busy crew.

Why it usually falls apart

  • "Everyone's job = nobody's job." A shared space with no single owner gets ignored.
  • Vague asks ("keep it clean") don't get done. Specific 60-second tasks do.
  • No visibility = no accountability. If no one sees who did it, it slides.

What actually works

  1. One owner at a time. A rotating weekly Shop Steward — that week, the shop/fridge is their buck to stop. Rotation posted so it's fair.
  2. Tiny, specific tasks. A checklist of one-minute items, not "clean up." Anyone can finish the list in 5 minutes.
  3. Tie it to clock-out. The day's list gets done before anyone leaves — same as gear gets put away. (Can hang off Branch Manager clock-out.)
  4. Make it visible. A shared checklist everyone can see — done or not done, no ambiguity.
  5. Restock is a running list. Anyone adds "we're low on ___"; the steward refills it. Nothing runs out by surprise.
  6. Reset weekly, reward lightly. Clean week → coffee/lunch is on Doug. Cheap, and it keeps it a team thing, not a nag.

What actually incentivizes it (the proven levers)

Keeping a shared space clean is a behavior problem, not a mop problem. These are the levers that reliably move it:

  1. Friendly competition — a "most / least" board. People respond to being seen. A simple weekly leaderboard of who did the most (and least) checklist items turns a chore into a game. (This is the same engine we'd use for the skate-shop clock-in/out app.)
  2. Small, immediate, frequent rewards beat big rare ones. "Clean week → coffee/lunch's on me Friday" lands harder than a vague year-end bonus. Reward the streak, not perfection.
  3. Make it social & visible. A shared, checked-off list creates gentle peer pressure — nobody wants to be the empty column. Private = ignored.
  4. One named owner. A rotating steward outperforms "everyone's responsible" every time — accountability needs a name on it.
  5. Streaks & team goals. "10 clean closes in a row = team lunch." Momentum is its own motivator; a broken streak stings just enough.
  6. The broken-windows effect. A clean space stays clean; the first mess invites the next. Reset daily and the standard holds itself.
