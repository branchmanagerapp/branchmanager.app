So the hydraulics are untested, not confirmed-damaged. Before committing even the $1,500: 15-min function check — lift a load, does it hold without drifting? Normal power/speed? Any whining or weeping at the seals? Smooth & strong = fine. While the head's apart, flush the hydraulic fluid + filter (cheap insurance).

The details

Full MTE mechanic diagnosis
  • Overheated again after a thermostat workaround → limped it back in.
  • Compression test: center cylinder down. Pulled the radiator cap — lots of compression in the cooling system. Funnel-on-cap test (let it boil and circulate): bubbles = combustion getting into the coolant → cracked head or head gasket. He's going with a hairline cracked head ("it runs cracked").
  • Mechanism: the glow-plug pre-chamber "pucks" (pressed into the head) warp/crack under heat and let compression leak past the compression ring into the coolant jackets. The oil jackets are separate and sealed — so no oil-in-coolant; engine isn't destroyed.
  • Cause: likely a plugged radiator originally → ran hot → cracked. Flipping the machine adds cylinder wear (oil pools at the top, hydrolocks; he pops the glow plugs and bumps it over to clear).
  • Why head-only is a band-aid: the center cylinder being down on compression means the bore is probably scored — a new head stops the overheating but won't fully restore power. Full power = a long block (Kubota's made these hard to rebuild).
  • The cheap fix: pull the head, new head + gasket set, valve job at his machine shop (Arnold's Garage, Canaan — ~$120 to set up the head), reinstall. He's mobile, ~1 hr out, hourly. ≈ $1,500 all in.
