The five things that actually decide this

1. You are on a Square plan that costs more than it saves verify

Your measured rate is 2.40% + 15¢ in person and 2.90% + 30¢ card-on-file. That is not Square's free-plan pricing — it matches Square Premium ($149/mo) exactly.

Premium's discount saves you $748/yr in fees at your volume. It costs $1,788/yr. You would need roughly $660,000/yr in card volume for it to break even. You did $276,185.

I confirmed the rate from per-transaction fee data. I could not confirm the plan through the API — Square exposes no merchant-plan endpoint. Check your Square dashboard before acting.

2. Processing fees are a wash not the lever

Applied to your real mix, every scenario lands within about $750/yr of the others on fees alone. Shopify Basic would cost you $15/yr more than what you pay Square today. That is noise. Stop weighing the platforms on processing rate — it does not move.

3. Shopify's cheap tier cannot run your shop 7 staff

Shopify caps staff by plan: Basic gives 2 seats (0 for stores opened after 2024), Grow 5, Advanced 15. POS Lite inherits that cap. Unlimited register PINs only arrive with POS Pro at $89/mo.

So the honest comparison is not "Shopify is $39." With seven people ringing sales it is $128/mo minimum — and at that price Square wins.

4. Square's commerce APIs are wide open verified live

I tested this against your account today. Catalog, Inventory, Orders and Payment Links all respond — you already have 11 payment links created. Only Square's website builder is closed; there is no pages or content API.

That distinction is the whole answer to your original question: Square will not let you rebuild its website, but it will happily sit behind yours.

5. Your Square readers do not work on Shopify one-time cost

Shopify POS does not talk to Square hardware. Moving means buying Shopify's readers, re-training the counter, and re-keying a 1,102-item catalog — during a season you are already running at $33k/month.

Hardware cost not verified — depends on how many stations you run. Budget it as a real one-time number before deciding, not as zero.

The one thing Shopify genuinely does better

Shopify lets you style the checkout page. On Square, the customer browses your site and then hands off to a Square-branded payment screen you cannot restyle. For 782 card-not-present transactions a year, that is a cosmetic cost — not $1,000/yr and a register migration.

What I would do, in order

1. Confirm the Square plan on your dashboard and downgrade if it is Premium — $1,040/yr, no downside at your volume.
2. Wire the shop page we already built to Square's Catalog and Payment Links APIs.
3. Revisit Shopify only if you cross roughly $660k in card volume or genuinely need a branded checkout.
