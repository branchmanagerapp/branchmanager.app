Chuck it down, or scale past it? — the honest answer

You asked the exact right question. Here's the straight version — and the one thing it all turns on: how much your equipment actually gets used.

A bucket truck or loader is only worth owning if it works enough days to beat renting or subbing it. A machine costing $30k/yr is cheap if it runs 200 days a year on booked work — and a money pit if it runs 40. Right now your fleet eats 36% of revenue, which usually means you're paying for iron that isn't busy enough. (I don't have your days-used numbers, so I can't tell you which it is — but that's the number that decides everything, and the calculator above lets you test it.)

Chuck it down (go lean)
Run a chip truck + chipper, do what you can solo, and sub out the crane/bucket removals (like the Osorio work). Slash the fixed fleet cost, keep more cash per job, sleep at night. Right if your big iron sits idle a lot.
Scale past it
Add crews and sales so the equipment runs constantly and the fixed cost spreads thin. Higher revenue, the iron pays for itself. Right only if you have the booked demand to keep it busy — and the stomach for more payroll + risk.
My honest read for where you are right now: you're cash-tight and already fixed-cost-heavy. Adding a loader payment on faith is the single move most likely to cause the exact cash panic you're trying to escape. Unless you have loader work already booked that covers its break-even (the calculator shows the number), the safer play is to hold off on the loader, fix or shed the worst-bleeding truck first, and grow the revenue before you add more iron. Scale when the work is forcing you to — not before. The worst seat is owning expensive equipment that's only half-used, and that's the trap the next payment walks you into.

This is analysis, not a guarantee — run the calculator with your real repair, payment, and days-used numbers and it'll tell you in dollars. I'd rather you decide on your numbers than my guesses.
