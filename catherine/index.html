Why this exists

Catherine is the whole business in one person

She runs field, equipment, logistics and office — staging trucks, fueling, saws, clocking crew, plus every estimate, schedule and client message. That's the #1 structural risk: too much lives in her head, and nothing runs without her.

Catherine AI isn't a fancy app. It's one job: get things out of her head and onto a screen — the day's priorities, her routines, and the follow-ups she'd otherwise write by hand — so a normal day takes less of her, and someone could step in if she's out.

Reduces key-person riskMobile-firstShe approves, AI drafts
Scope · the three things it does

What Catherine AI does

  • 1. Today's suggestions — an AI-triaged shortlist: aging estimates first (storm quotes decay in ~48h), then agreed money, then callbacks owed. So the important thing never hides behind an old one.
  • 2. Her routines as checklists — morning truck/equipment prep and end-of-day shutdown, tap-through, saved on her phone, reset daily. The routine lives in the tool, not only in her memory — so it's teachable/coverable.
  • 3. Drafted office work — AI writes the follow-up text, the client reply, the "done job → invoice" — she reviews and taps send. It does the typing; she keeps the judgment.

Non-negotiable

Guardrails

  • AI never sends a client message on its own — everything lands in a draft queue Catherine (or Doug) approves and sends. Same rule as the rest of Branch Manager.
  • No automated marketing/email firing — this is a helper, not an autopilot.
  • AI never invents a number, price, or contact — it works from real records or says it doesn't know.
How it's wired

Where the data comes from

  • Real Branch Manager records — open quotes / jobs / invoices / clients. Suggestions should pull from these, not be hand-maintained forever.
  • Messages & calls — missed calls and texts become "callbacks owed." (Deep-search caveat: some texts live only in the message BLOB — the tool must read those too, or it'll miss things.)
  • Her routines — the AM/PM checklists, editable so they match how she actually works.
Reality check: a genuinely useful v1 can start hand-curated by Claude (like Doug's Today sheet) and only later wire to live BM data. Don't gate the whole thing on the integration.
Sequence

Phases — ship small, grow what earns it

Phase 1
The page: suggestions + checklists
Mobile page on her phone. Today's shortlist + AM/PM routines that save locally. Content kept current (hand-curated to start). A real, finished thing.
Phase 2
Wire to live Branch Manager data
Suggestions pull from actual open quotes/jobs/invoices instead of being maintained by hand.
Phase 3
Draft queue
One-tap AI-drafted follow-ups & replies into an approve-and-send queue.
Phase 4
Voice capture
Catherine speaks a job in the field; AI turns it into a quote draft. Only if the earlier phases prove out.
The whole point of phasing: Phase 1 is useful on day one and is a single clean prompt. Nothing else is committed until it earns its place.
Decide before building

Open questions

Answer these and Phase 1 becomes one prompt instead of weeks of rework.
1. Is this its own page, or part of Ground Control / the Today board?
There's already a shared Doug+Catherine command center and a Today board in the app. Catherine AI could be a new page or a section of what exists — decide so we don't build a duplicate.
2. Does Catherine actually want this — and in what form?
Her buy-in decides everything. A tool she didn't ask for gets ignored. What would genuinely help her vs. feel like extra admin?
3. Hand-curated or live-data for v1?
Curated ships this week; live-BM-data is more build. Recommend curated first.
4. What are her real routines, in her words?
The AM/PM checklists should be exactly her steps, not my guess. Get the actual list from her.
5. Where does the draft queue live?
In Branch Manager (with the other drafts) or standalone? Affects Phase 3.
6. Private to Doug+Catherine, or does she own it?
Who updates it, who sees it — sets whether it needs a login/gate.
Build target

Once these are answered → one prompt

  1. Confirm form + get Catherine's real routines — answer the six above with her.
  2. Lock Phase 1 scope — the three things, hand-curated to start.
  3. One clean prompt builds the page — this doc is the spec it references.
  4. She uses it for a week, then we wire live data — grow only if it stuck.
