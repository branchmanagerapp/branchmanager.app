The CapCut playbook: iPhone + GoPro → posted.

A start-to-finish workflow for turning raw phone and GoPro footage into clean marketing clips — skate reels, tree before/afters, brand promos. Free, runs on your Mac, nothing to build. Follow it top to bottom the first few times; after that it's muscle memory.

CapCut runs great on your Intel Sequoia machine — no hardware change, no subscription for the core editor. Grab the desktop app from capcut.com (and the iPhone app from the App Store for quick edits on the go).

A Get your footage in one place

01

Make one project folder before you touch CapCut

Everything editing starts with knowing where the clips are. Make a folder per shoot — e.g. ~/Desktop/Footage/2026-07-04 Tree Job — and pull both cameras into it.

  • iPhone: AirDrop the clips to the Mac, or let iCloud Photos sync and drag them out of Photos into the folder.
  • GoPro: pop the SD card into a reader (or use the GoPro Quik app) and copy the .MP4 files over. GoPro also drops .LRV/.THM sidecar files — ignore those, you only need the MP4s.

B The edit — step by step

02

New project & import

Open CapCut → New project. Drag your whole shoot folder into the Media panel (top-left). Then drag the clips you want down onto the timeline at the bottom, in rough order.

03

Set your shape first do this early

Pick the aspect ratio before you fine-tune, so you're framing for the real output. Top toolbar → Ratio:

  • 9:16 vertical — Reels, TikTok, Shorts, Stories. This is most of your marketing.
  • 16:9 horizontal — YouTube, website hero, before/after showcases.
  • 1:1 square — feed posts if you want them.
04

Rough cut — kill the dead weight

Move the white playhead to where a bad take starts, hit the Split tool (the scissors icon above the timeline), split again where it ends, click the junk chunk and Delete. Repeat until only the good stuff is left. Drag clip edges inward to trim tighter. Don't polish yet — just get to a clean spine.

05

Tighten & add motion

  • Speed: select a clip → Speed panel. Slow-mo a trick landing or a saw cut; speed up the boring setup 2–4×.
  • Stabilize shaky GoPro/handheld: select clip → Video → Stabilization.
  • Transitions: keep it simple — a hard cut on the beat beats a fancy wipe. Add sparingly from the Transitions tab between clips.
06

Auto-captions the killer feature

If anyone's talking: Text → Auto captions → generate. CapCut transcribes the whole thing in seconds. Always read them back — it'll mishear names and jargon (“Peekskill,” tree species, “chipper”). Double-click any caption to fix it, then style once and it applies to all. Captions matter: most people watch social with sound off.

07

Sound

  • Music: Audio tab has a royalty-free library — pick a track, drag it under your clips, trim to length.
  • Duck the music under talking: lower the music clip's Volume so voices cut through.
  • GoPro wind noise? Select the clip's audio → Reduce noise.
08

Titles & brand

Text → Add text for a hook in the first second (“60ft Oak Removal — Peekskill”) and a call-to-action at the end. Drop your logo in via Media if you want it cornered. Keep it clean — one hook, one CTA.

09

Export

Top-right Export. Match the preset table below, keep format MP4, then upload. That's a posted clip.

C GoPro-specific fixes

Flat / washed-out color

GoPro footage looks dull raw. Select clip → Adjust → nudge Contrast + Saturation up a touch, or drop a light Filter. Don't overcook it.

Fisheye warp

Wide-angle bends straight lines. If it bugs you, GoPro's Quik app can flatten it on export, or crop in slightly in CapCut.

Match the frame rate

Shooting GoPro at 60fps for slow-mo but iPhone at 30? Fine to mix — just export at 30fps so playback is consistent.

Big files

GoPro 4K clips are huge. Copy them to the Mac's drive first; editing off the SD card is slow and risks dropouts.

E Where I plug in

I can't move CapCut's timeline for you, but I do everything around the edit so you sit down to assembly, not a blank screen:
  • Pull the best clips from your skate pipeline and hand you a shortlist to drop in.
  • Write the words — hooks, on-screen titles, captions, CTAs, and the post caption + hashtags per brand.
  • Batch-organize footage into dated project folders so step 01 is already done.
  • Repurpose one edit into per-brand versions (tree / skate / SmartLawn).
The one-line version One folder → import → set the shape → cut the junk → auto-caption → music → hook + CTA → export MP4. Do that loop a few times and you're posting in under an hour a clip.
Footnote — the tools we didn't pick

Palmier (the agent-driven editor where Claude edits the timeline directly) is the one to watch, but it needs macOS 26 Tahoe + Apple Silicon — it can't run on this Intel Mac. Park it until/unless you're on new hardware for other reasons.

Descript is worth a look if you start doing talking-head / testimonial videos — it lets you edit video by editing the transcript like a doc. DaVinci Resolve (free) is the pro step-up if you ever outgrow CapCut. Building our own (“PopCut”) stays shelved — a video editor is a huge build and not worth the debt.
