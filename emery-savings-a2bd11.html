Emery's savings: custodial account or a trust?

The goal is general savings in Emery's name. Here is how the two options work under New York and federal rules, so we can decide together. It's background reading, not legal or tax advice; a lawyer should look before anything is signed.

The short version

A custodial account (UTMA) is quick, free to open and simple. The money legally belongs to Emery from day one, and in New York it becomes fully hers at 21 (or 18, if the person giving chooses that when the account is set up). After that she decides how to use it.

A trust costs a lawyer's fee to set up and takes more paperwork, but the person creating it writes the rules: when Emery gets the money, in what amounts, and for what. It can keep control well past 21.

The deciding question is mostly: are we comfortable with Emery having full control of this money at 21? If yes, a custodial account is the simple choice. If no, or if the amount is large, a trust is worth the cost.

Option 1

Custodial account (UTMA)

Who owns it
Emery. An adult (the custodian) manages it for her. Gifts can't be taken back.
When she gets it
Age 21 in New York, or 18 if chosen at setup.
Cost to set up
Free at most brokers. Robinhood offers them; the custodian needs their own Robinhood account.
What it can be spent on
Anything for Emery's benefit (school, medical, camp). Not the custodian's own bills or normal parenting costs.
Investments
Stocks, funds, cash. No margin or borrowing.
Option 2

Trust for Emery

Who owns it
The trust. A trustee (could be Grandpa or Doug) follows the written rules.
When she gets it
Whenever the trust says: 25, 30, in stages, or only for certain uses.
Cost to set up
A lawyer's fee to draft it, plus a tax return each year in many setups. Ask a New York lawyer for a quote.
What it can be spent on
Whatever the trust document allows.
Investments
Whatever the trustee chooses, within the rules.

The $2,700 kiddie-tax line is twice the IRS's 2026 figure of $1,350. Exact tax depends on the year and Emery's other income; check with Kevin, the family's accountant.

A third option worth knowing about

Trump Accounts (new in 2026) are a kind of IRA for children. Family can put in up to $5,000 a year combined until the year she turns 17. Earnings grow tax-deferred. The money generally can't come out before 18, and then it becomes Emery's own IRA. Kids born 2025–2028 get a $1,000 government deposit. It's a long-term account, not general savings, but it can sit alongside either option. Details: trumpaccounts.gov.

Questions to bring to a lawyer
  1. Roughly how much will go in, and does that size justify a trust's cost?
  2. What age, or what conditions, do we want before Emery has full control?
  3. Who should be trustee or custodian, and who is the backup?
  4. If a trust: revocable or irrevocable, and who pays its taxes?
  5. How does each choice affect college aid?
