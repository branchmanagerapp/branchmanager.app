Second Nature Tree Service ✕ BrinkStar

Working Together — The Options

One team where each of us does what we're best at. Merit-based, written down, and accountable — so trust never has to carry what a ledger can.

What's true in every option

  • Health first. Andrew's only job right now is recovery. Everything below runs without needing him on a job site.
  • Clients stay cared for. Every BrinkStar client gets serviced under the relationship Andrew and Michelle built — nothing falls apart, nobody gets poached.
  • Every dollar is documented. Referred jobs, equipment days, and hours are tracked in Branch Manager. A monthly statement shows exactly what was earned and why.
  • Merit decides everything. Contribution is recorded, not remembered. Whatever structure we grow into, the record is the basis.
  • Nobody inherits old obligations. Each company keeps its own past. We combine forward, not backward.

The options on the table

Option 3 · Start now — happens in every scenario

Stewardship — Second Nature takes over until Andrew's back on his feet

What's already happening, written down. Second Nature runs BrinkStar's book while Andrew recovers; everything is reversible.

  • Clients: jobs Michelle or Andrew forward are tagged "Brink-referred" in Branch Manager and pay BrinkStar ___% of the job, settled monthly with a statement.
  • Equipment: mini skid, trailer, chipper earn $___/day whenever Second Nature runs them.
  • Crew: BrinkStar's good people go onto Second Nature payroll (real W-2, workers' comp) so nobody loses a season.
  • When Andrew's ready: everything can revert, or roll into Option 1 or 2 — with months of real numbers to price it from.

Paperwork: one page. Risk: near zero. This is the right move no matter which door we pick later — and it makes the later choice smarter.

Option 1 · The buyout — owner-financed

Second Nature buys BrinkStar's business over time

Second Nature acquires the assets — equipment, client list, phone number, brand, goodwill — paid monthly over ___ months from the revenue those assets produce. The business pays for itself; Andrew gets a dependable income stream through recovery and beyond.

  • Structure it as an asset purchase, not buying the company itself — that keeps old company obligations where they belong and makes the deal clean for both sides.
  • Price: agreed valuation of gear + client book; a stewardship period first (Option 3) makes the client-book value real instead of guessed.
  • Andrew's ongoing role is separate and additive — development work under Option 2-style merit pay if he wants it.

Best if: Andrew wants out of ownership and steady money. Watch-out: pricing the client book fairly before stewardship data exists.

Option 2 · Equity — earned by the ledger

Andrew earns ownership working merit-based

Andrew works the development role and earns into ownership on a written schedule tied to documented contribution — the Branch Manager ledger of who sourced, sold, worked, and supplied every job.

  • The honest structural note: Second Nature Tree LLC also owns the software and other lines. Equity should live in the tree/storm operating business (a profit interest or a new operating entity both brands roll into) — so what Andrew earns matches what Andrew builds.
  • Vesting by record, not by time: milestones like contracts landed and revenue sourced, straight off the monthly statements.
  • Fully compatible with Option 1 — buy the assets now, earn equity in the combined operation after.

Best if: Andrew wants to build long-term, not exit. Watch-out: never grant fixed percentages up front — fixed equity freezes merit.

Option 4 · Per-contract joint venture

Team up deal-by-deal on the big work

No standing structure — each storm, utility, or municipal pursuit gets its own written split: Second Nature's insurance, payroll and production; Andrew's relationships and hustle. Settle per contract.

Best if: we want to prove the partnership on real wins before committing to anything permanent. Lightest possible commitment; also the least momentum.

Option 5 · The sequenced path (recommended shape)

3 → then 1 or 2, priced by the record

Start stewardship this week. Run 3–6 months of documented operation. Then choose: buyout (Option 1) if Andrew wants steady money and freedom, or earn-in equity (Option 2) if he wants to build — with the ledger, not a hospital-bed guess, setting the numbers either way.

This isn't indecision — it's sequencing. Every serious option gets stronger with stewardship data in front of it.
