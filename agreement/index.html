Founding Agreement

The plain-language deal between founding members of our tree cooperative. Read it, agree it together, then it's finalized with a co-op attorney + CPA and signed by all.

We're forming one worker-owned cooperative so we can pool costs, be fully legitimate and covered, and build real equity together. Each founding member is an equal owner. This is what each of us agrees to:

  1. One cooperative, equal owners. We operate as a single worker-owned co-op. Each founding member is an owner.
  2. One member, one vote. Equal say in how it's run, regardless of size or buy-in.
  3. Buy-in. Each member contributes [buy-in amount] — cash and/or valued equipment — to become an owner. Ownership vests after a [probation period].
  4. All tree work goes through the co-op. Booked, billed, insured, and shared through the co-op. No side jobs using co-op leads, gear, insurance, or name. Pre-existing personal clients are listed up front; family/out-of-trade favors allowed with disclosure.
  5. Pay & rates. Each member is paid an agreed day-rate/wage for work performed, at standard industry rates by role. Rates are set and changed only by group vote — no one raises their own pay.
  6. Profit sharing. Year-end surplus is shared by contribution (patronage), after the co-op keeps its reserves.
  7. Equipment. Routine maintenance comes from a co-op reserve; operators care for gear daily; damage from negligence or misuse is on the responsible member.
  8. Bidding. Every job clears the co-op price floor and target margin before booking. Consistent estimating, no lowballing.
  9. Fair work. Members take their share of the less-desirable jobs, not just the big ones.
  10. Employees. The co-op may hire W-2 staff at fair wages with benefits — not ownership — with a path to membership over time.
  11. Clients & leads. Belong to the co-op. Non-solicit of co-op clients if a member leaves.
  12. Decisions. An ops lead handles day-to-day; big calls need a supermajority; a written dispute step before anyone walks.
  13. Books & legitimacy. All revenue on the books, open to members, one CPA. No off-book cash, ever.
  14. Losses & reserves. The co-op keeps a cash reserve; small losses come from it. A year in the red beyond reserve is shared by contribution and reduces each member's capital account. If cash is genuinely needed, members may face a capital call for their share.
  15. Pay your share, or forfeit. Each member owes their share — buy-in installments, their portion of a loss or capital call, or negligent-damage costs. Unpaid amounts are debited from the member's equity (capital account). If equity is exhausted and the member still owes or won't contribute, they forfeit membership — after written notice and a chance to cure, by group vote.
  16. Leaving. A departing member's capital account is bought out on a set formula, paid over time; clear rules for death/disability; non-solicit on the way out.
By signing, each founding member agrees to:
  • Put all tree work through the co-op and follow the side-work rule
  • Work at the agreed rate and meet the bidding price floor
  • Care for shared equipment and own up to negligent damage
  • Keep everything on the books and act in the co-op's interest
