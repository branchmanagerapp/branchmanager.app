Todd + the Intrepid play

Two ways we back each other up as I stand up the Northeast dealership — and both of them work in your favor.

Way 1 — Demo your KM130 at the shows

Show the big machine without me buying one

I'm anchoring the shows with my own KM100 — but buyers want to see the step-up 130 too (farms, estates, heavier material). Instead of me buying a 130 I don't need yet, I bring yours out and put it to work in front of the exact buyers who'd want one.

Way 2 — You're the inventory backstop

The machine you wanted anyway — at a better price

The one real risk in a new dealership is bringing in a unit that doesn't sell. You solve that — because you've been wanting your own machine regardless.

  • I bring in a demo / consignment unit. If it moves, great.
  • If it doesn't sell, you buy it — at the demo / dealer pricing I can get you, well under what you'd pay cold retail. price to confirm w/ Chris
  • You end up with the machine you wanted, cheaper — and I clear the floor with near-zero risk, which is exactly what lets me stock inventory confidently in year one.

Win-win in the truest sense: your backstop is what makes my inventory possible, and being the backstop is how you get your machine below retail.

What's in it for you

  • A machine at demo/dealer pricing — the backstop role gets you in under retail.
  • Local service & parts for your Intrepid — Ron (mobile mechanic) + Kevin's shop + a parts bench right here, instead of shipping a part across the country and waiting. That's real value for the 130 you already run.
  • Your 130 in front of buyers — free exposure, and a cut if it helps close a sale.
  • A real Intrepid presence in the Northeast that you helped start — better support for every Intrepid owner in the region, you included.

What I'm asking

  1. Demo your KM130 at the two 2026 shows — I do the heavy lifting, you lend the machine.
  2. Be the backstop buyer on a unit if it doesn't sell — at the good pricing, the machine you wanted anyway.

Nothing binding today — just a handshake so when the shows and the inventory come, we're lined up. We work out the finder's cut and the backstop price fair, in plain numbers, before anything's real.
