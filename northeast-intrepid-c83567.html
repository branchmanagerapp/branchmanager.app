The Intrepid line sells itself once a contractor sees it work — but most of the Northeast has no one to demo it, deliver it, or fix it. Northeast Intrepid fills that gap: a single, accountable regional partner that gets machines in front of buyers, into their hands for a real demo, and keeps them running after the sale.

Territory

Initial focus: the lower Hudson Valley and Western Connecticut — Westchester, Putnam, Dutchess, Orange, Rockland, and Fairfield County — a dense market of tree, landscape, excavation, and farm operators with no nearby Intrepid presence. Demo center located centrally on this corridor, with delivery and demos radiating out. Room to expand coverage north and east as volume supports it.

The demo center base sits in the Route 9 / Albany Post Road corridor in Cortlandt / Peekskill — and it's a third of a mile from Jim Reed's (Jim Reed Truck Sales / Bobcat of Westchester, 5742 Albany Post Rd). That matters:

  • We slot in alongside that traffic, not against it — Intrepid loaders aren't what Jim Reed sells; we add the compact-loader piece the same crowd needs.
  • It's a proven equipment destination — putting the Northeast's first Intrepid demo center here means buyers don't have to be taught where to go.

How the floor-plan model works for both of us

The whole plan hinges on inventory I can show and demo without buying it outright up front. The structure Chris floated does exactly that:

StageWhat happens
Months 0–6Machines on the floor / in the demo fleet. No payments due — time to get them in front of buyers and sold.
Months 6–12Payments begin; balance paid down as units sell, due in full by month 12.
Consignment (ideal)Unsold units I can return rather than owe on — so a slow stretch never sinks me, and you're never stuck with a partner who can't pay.

This is the lowest-risk way to stand up a real demo center: it lets me carry enough inventory to demo and deliver immediately, while my exposure stays tied to actual sales. terms above per Chris — to be confirmed in writing
