Northeast Intrepid

A dedicated regional home for Intrepid loaders in the Northeast — demo center, demos, delivery, and real service behind every machine.

The Intrepid line sells itself once a contractor sees it work — but most of the Northeast has no one to demo it, deliver it, or fix it. Northeast Intrepid fills that gap: a single, accountable regional partner that gets machines in front of buyers, into their hands for a real demo, and keeps them running after the sale.

Chris has proven the machine moves — by his own count, roughly 150 units over 7–8 years at 20–30 a year, almost entirely on reputation and word of mouth. The public record backs the trajectory: Intrepid has been in the U.S. since 2018, sold nationwide, and the KM100 Tele has caught on with tree-care companies — compact, with telescoping reach and articulated steering that won't tear up a lawn, which is exactly my world. That's a strong base built without a physical demo center, a demo program, or a local service bench in the Northeast — the things that turn a "maybe" into a sale and a one-time buyer into a referral source. Northeast Intrepid is built to add that layer, not to compete with it.

What Northeast Intrepid does

Six things, all run off one simple by-appointment site — a buyer picks what they need and a time, and we take it from there.

Delivery

We bring the machine to the jobsite or yard — no buyer has to arrange freight or pickup.

Working demos

On-site demos on real work — loading logs, moving material, tight-space maneuvering. Not a parking-lot walkaround.

Demo center

A real place to see, sit in, and compare machines, attachments, and options.

Rentals

Rent a unit by the day or week — try it on a real job before buying, or just get the work done.

Service & parts

Local mechanics for setup, warranty work, and repairs — the piece that's been missing in the region.

Trade & advice

Trade-ins, financing guidance, and honest fit advice from someone who runs this equipment daily.

Why me, why now

Doug Brown — Second Nature Tree Service (Peekskill, NY). I run a full tree service operation in the Hudson Valley, so I'm not a salesman learning the product — I'm the customer. I buy, run, and beat on this class of machine every week, and my own crews are the first proof-of-concept demo.

  • A demo yard built for it — we run a small firewood yard, so there are always logs on-site to load, plus a mulch, dirt, and gravel demo area and a whole hillside a buyer can dig into when they want to really lean on the machine. I can put an Intrepid to work on the actual job, in front of the exact buyers who'd want one — tree, landscape, and farm operators across the Tri-State area (New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut).
  • An existing network of contractors and the back-office systems to run scheduling, invoicing, and a fleet — the bones of a dealership are already in place.
  • Credibility with buyers — they trust a recommendation from someone who owns and depends on the machine — more than a sales rep.

We're serious — here's the commitment

This isn't a tire-kicker asking you to float machines. Before I ask you for any inventory, I'm putting my own money and my whole operation behind it:

  • I'm buying my own KM100 first — my own money on the line. It's my anchor demo unit and my skin in the game. You're not backing a dealer who hasn't bought one himself.
  • Service is already lined up — two committed local equipment partners (named below), with service agreements drafted.
  • I have the yard, the trucks, and the customers today — a working tree service operation that runs this class of machine every week.

All I'm asking you to carry is consignment inventory I can return — the rest of the risk is already on me.

Service partners — the missing piece, solved

A loader only earns its keep when it's running — downtime is what kills its value. The reason buyers hesitate on an imported machine is service. Northeast Intrepid answers that up front with two committed local partners:

  • Kevin Christiana — Kevin's Heavy Trucking. Heavy hauling, transport, and equipment service — covers machine delivery and the bigger iron. Kevin already does a lot of the truck upfits for Jim Reed's next door, plus heavy contractor trucking and heavy-truck repair — so we sit right in the middle of that network, with good relationships on both sides.
  • Ron Kopyscianski — Ron's Rescue Repair. Certified small-engine, powersports & equipment mechanic (Lake Peekskill). My go-to wrench — engine, hydraulic, and setup work.

Between them they cover pre-delivery setup & inspection, warranty and repair work handled locally instead of shipping a machine across the country, and parts support & routine maintenance to keep buyers running and loyal.

How the floor-plan model works for both of us

The whole plan hinges on inventory I can show and demo without buying it outright up front. The structure Chris floated does exactly that:

StageWhat happens
Months 0–6Machines on the floor / in the demo fleet. No payments due — time to get them in front of buyers and sold.
Months 6–12Payments begin; balance paid down as units sell, due in full by month 12.
Consignment (ideal)Unsold units I can return rather than owe on — so a slow stretch never sinks me, and you're never stuck with a partner who can't pay.

Of these, consignment is the version I'm really after (see the ask below) — I pay you back out of sales rather than out of pocket, since I don't have the capital to start floor-plan payments at month six. Straight talk

I'll be straight with you, Chris. On paper, a bigger multi-brand dealer — someone already carrying Giant, New Holland, or Kubota — might make more sense than one tree guy putting a few pieces together. I get that, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

But here's where I think I more than make up for it — and move machines you wouldn't otherwise:

  • I'll sell these because I genuinely like them. The interest is real — and guys can tell the difference between a sales pitch and someone who actually wants the machine. That's most of selling right there.
  • I'm already out there. I'm driving around the Northeast meeting other tree guys — honestly, it sounds like a good time, and relationships are how this equipment actually moves.
  • The location. Being a third of a mile from Jim Reed's and Bobcat puts us right where the contractors already are — that's the piece a bigger dealer three hours away just doesn't have.
  • A small independent fits this brand. Intrepid is simple, workable iron for people who actually run it — an owner's brand, a tree guy's brand, a worker's brand. That's better served by a local guy who helps owners keep their machines running than by a big, locked-down dealer network (the John Deere model, where you practically can't fix your own machine). Me being small isn't a weakness here — it's the same ethos as the product.

No smoke. I know what I am and what I'm not. I think the mix of real interest, a willingness to learn the wrench side, boots on the ground, and that location is worth betting on.

What I'm really asking for

  1. Consignment inventory — the core of the ask. Put units here on your terms: you decide what to place, how many, and you can pull a machine back or move it somewhere else whenever you want. I demo and sell them; whatever doesn't move, you take back.
  2. I pay you out of sales, not out of pocket. Concretely: 100% of the profit from the first sale goes straight into buying in the next floor model — then half the profit of every sale after that goes to floor-model capital until the demo fleet is bought in. The business bootstraps its own inventory instead of needing capital I don't have up front. exact split open — your floor-plan terms still welcome where they fit
  3. A Northeast dealer / distribution relationship — Northeast Intrepid as the region's demo center, delivery, and service partner for the Intrepid line.
  4. Dealer pricing / margin and parts access — the per-unit economics and a parts pipeline to back the service operation.

In short: you've already proven the machine sells in the Northeast on word of mouth alone. Front me consignment inventory on terms you control — return or move anything that isn't working — and let me pay you back out of what sells. Low risk on your side, real presence and service on mine, and we both move more machines to buyers who stay buyers. I know it's a big ask. I'm asking it straight.
