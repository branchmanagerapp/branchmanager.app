Northeast Intrepid

A dedicated regional home for Intrepid loaders in the Northeast — demo center, demos, delivery, and real service behind every machine.
Prepared for Chris Slarenk · Intrepid importer · June 2026

The Intrepid line sells itself once a contractor sees it work — but most of the Northeast has no one to demo it, deliver it, or fix it. Northeast Intrepid fills that gap: a single, accountable regional partner that gets machines in front of buyers, into their hands for a real demo, and keeps them running after the sale.

Chris has proven the machine moves — roughly 150 units over 7–8 years at 20–30 a year, almost entirely on reputation and word of mouth. That's a strong base built without a physical demo center, a demo program, or a local service bench in the Northeast. Those are exactly the things that turn a "maybe" into a sale and a one-time buyer into a referral source. Northeast Intrepid is built to add that layer, not to compete with it.

Why me, why now

Doug Brown — Second Nature Tree Service (Peekskill, NY). I run a full tree and land-clearing operation in the Hudson Valley, so I'm not a salesman learning the product — I'm the customer. I buy, run, and beat on this class of machine every week, and my own crews are the first proof-of-concept demo.

  • Real demo yard + real work — I can show the machine doing the job, on actual sites, to the exact buyers who'd want one (tree, landscape, excavation, and farm operators across the Hudson Valley / Westchester / Putnam / Fairfield corridor).
  • An existing network of contractors and a back-office platform (Branch Manager) I already use to run scheduling, invoicing, and fleet — the bones of a dealership are already in place.
  • Credibility with buyers — they trust a recommendation from someone who owns and depends on the machine, not a sales rep.

Service partners — the missing piece, solved

A loader is only worth what it's worth when it's down. The reason buyers hesitate on an imported machine is service. Northeast Intrepid answers that up front with two experienced equipment mechanics — Ron and Kevin — as service and warranty partners:

  • Pre-delivery setup & inspection so every machine leaves right.
  • Warranty and repair work handled locally instead of shipping a machine across the country.
  • Parts support and routine maintenance to keep buyers running and loyal.

Final structure (employee vs. contracted shop vs. revenue-share) to be confirmed with Ron and Kevin.

Territory

Initial focus: the lower Hudson Valley and Western Connecticut — Westchester, Putnam, Dutchess, Orange, Rockland, and Fairfield County — a dense market of tree, landscape, excavation, and farm operators with no nearby Intrepid presence. Demo center located centrally on this corridor, with delivery and demos radiating out. Room to expand coverage north and east as volume supports it.

How the floor-plan model works for both of us

The whole plan hinges on inventory I can show and demo without buying it outright up front. The structure Chris floated does exactly that:

StageWhat happens
Months 0–6Machines on the floor / in the demo fleet. No payments due — time to get them in front of buyers and sold.
Months 6–12Payments begin; balance paid down as units sell, due in full by month 12.
Consignment (ideal)Unsold units I can return rather than owe on — so a slow stretch never sinks me, and you're never stuck with a partner who can't pay.

This is the lowest-risk way to stand up a real demo center: it lets me carry enough inventory to demo and deliver immediately, while my exposure stays tied to actual sales. terms above per Chris — to be confirmed in writing

What I'm asking Chris for

  1. A Northeast dealer / distribution relationship — Northeast Intrepid as the region's demo center, delivery, and service partner for the Intrepid line.
  2. Floor-plan inventory on the terms discussed — 6 months before payments start, paid in full by month 12 — so I can stand up a real demo fleet without buying it all up front.
  3. A consignment arrangement on unsold units (return rather than owe), so neither of us is exposed if a quarter runs slow.
  4. Dealer pricing / margin and parts access — the per-unit economics and a parts pipeline to back the service operation.

In short: you've already proven the machine sells in the Northeast on word of mouth alone. Give it a real storefront, a demo program, and a service bench — and let me carry the inventory risk through a floor plan — and we both sell more of them, with less friction, to buyers who stay buyers.
