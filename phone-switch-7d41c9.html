Moving the business phone from Dialpad to Twilio

What changes, what Catherine will notice, how the switch day goes, and where Jobber fits in.

The short version

  • Twilio is the phone company. Branch Manager is the screen where calls, texts and voicemails show up. So it's Dialpad → Twilio, and Twilio feeds Branch Manager. Those aren't two different options; it's one setup.
  • The number customers call, (914) 391-5233, stays the same. Customers notice nothing.
  • For Catherine the big change: calls ring her normal cell phone instead of the Dialpad app, and she presses 1 to answer.
  • There is no hour-long outage. The number keeps working on Dialpad until the moment it moves. The risky part is a short window on switch day, so we pick a quiet time.
  • Jobber doesn't connect to Dialpad or Twilio on its own. But because we own the Twilio setup, we can send calls into Jobber ourselves (see the Jobber section).

Switch day: how the move goes

There isn't a long outage. Porting is a paperwork process between phone companies that takes about 5–15 days. The whole time, 391-5233 keeps working normally on Dialpad. On the scheduled day and time, the number flips to Twilio.

  1. Before: Doug gets the port PIN from Dialpad and a recent Dialpad bill. We submit the port to Twilio, and Doug signs the e-form Twilio emails.
  2. Pick the flip time: Twilio lets us choose the date and time. Choose a quiet one, like a winter weekday evening or a weekend morning.
  3. The flip: calls start arriving through Twilio and ringing Catherine's cell the new way. For a short window, some calls may still land on Dialpad, so Catherine keeps the Dialpad app open that day.
  4. Texts right after the flip: we attach the number to the approved texting registration right away. Until that's done, texts sent from BM on the main number can fail (they show as failed in BM, never silently).
  5. Right after: test a call in, a text in, a text out, and a voicemail. Then point BM's sending number at 391-5233.
  6. Only then cancel Dialpad, well before the 3/4/2027 renewal. Dialpad itself says not to cancel until the port is complete.
Safety net: if anything looks wrong on switch day, Dialpad is still paid for, and the number can be pointed at Doug's cell from the Twilio side in about a minute.

What about Jobber?

Right: Jobber doesn't plug into Dialpad or Twilio. Jobber's only built-in phone connection is Quo (formerly OpenPhone). There's a paid third-party app, KodaAI, that can listen to Dialpad calls and create Jobber requests.

But we don't need either. Branch Manager already talks to Jobber: it can create Jobber clients and requests through Jobber's API, the same way website leads go in. Since every Twilio call, text and voicemail already passes through our own code, we can add one step: a new caller or a voicemail creates a draft request in Jobber, with the transcript in the notes and tagged "- Claude", so Catherine sees it where she already works.

Option Not built. It's a small add-on once Doug OKs it, and it never texts or emails the customer.
