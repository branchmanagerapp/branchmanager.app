Why it feels like it's failing

1. Profit ≠ cash in the bank

"$27k profit" is not $27k sitting in the account. Profit is a year-end scoreboard; cash is what's there on a Tuesday, and it moves constantly. You can be profitable and still stare at an empty account. That gap is the whole problem — and it's fixable.

2. Your revenue is lumpy; your bills are not

Real 2025: from ~2% of the year in slow months (Jan, Aug) to ~18% in September. But truck payments and insurance hit every month the same. Slow stretches bleed; big months you're just catching up. That's the scarcity feeling — timing, not failure.

3. You DID take money out — it just didn't feel like a paycheck

The books show $17,781 in owner draws in 2025 — $14,000 in cash counter-withdrawals plus ~$3,800 of personal charges on the card. You pulled ~$18k; it dribbled out as scattered cash, not a salary, so it vanished into life.

Added to the $17,781 above, you actually pulled ~$33,217 out of the business in 2025. You DID pay yourself ~$33k — it just came as scattered cash and transfers, so it never felt like a paycheck.

The real kicker: you took ~$33,217 out while the business earned ~$26,964 (normalized) — more than it made — so the bank shrank. That ~$33k WAS your pay. The problems: it was invisible (no steady paycheck), it topped what the business actually earned, and it's short of a real target wage. Fix those three and the same money feels like income instead of a leak.
