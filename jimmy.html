Jimmy — three ways we can keep doing this.

You're running my bucket truck, chipper and chip truck a few days a month and it's been working. Here's how I see the options going forward, with the honest upside and downside of each. Pick what fits and we'll put it on paper.

Where we are today. Those numbers are below what a rental yard charges for the same iron, which is part of why I want to talk about this.

1. Keep renting by the day

Same as now, but done properly.

You book the equipment, pay a day rate, run your own jobs on it. Simple, and you already know how it works.

What has to change: my insurance covers people and property I hurt while using the gear. It does not cover the gear itself while it's in someone else's hands, and it doesn't cover damage from misuse. So for this to continue I need, every time:

  • A certificate of insurance naming Second Nature Tree Service as additional insured
  • Proof you carry rented or borrowed equipment coverage
  • A signed rental agreement with a damage clause — if it breaks on your job, you own the repair

Good for you

  • Total independence — your jobs, your customers, your invoice
  • No change to how you price

Hard for you

  • You pay whether the day is big or small
  • You carry the equipment risk and the extra insurance
  • Rates will need to move closer to market

2. Percentage of the job

You only pay when you earn.

Instead of a day rate, I take a percentage of the gross on any job you run with my equipment. Somewhere in the twenty to thirty percent range depending on which pieces go out. Nothing owed on a day the truck sits or a job falls through.

Fuel, maintenance and equipment insurance stay with me. You still work under your own name and invoice your own customers.

Good for you

  • Zero cost on slow or rained-out days
  • No equipment insurance to buy
  • Bigger jobs are where the margin is anyway

Hard for you

  • You share the upside on a big job
  • I'll need to see the job total, so it runs on trust and clean paperwork
What I'd actually push for

3. Run the jobs under Second Nature

You lead the crew, I carry the company.

The job gets sold and invoiced through Second Nature Tree Service. My equipment, my insurance, my liability, my name on the contract. You run it as a subcontractor or crew lead and get paid a cut of the job or a set day rate — whichever we agree on.

This is the only version where the insurance question just goes away, because my policy is written for exactly this: my iron, on my jobs, run by my people.

To do it right you'd need to be either a legitimate subcontractor with your own general liability and workers comp, or on the books as an employee. Blurring that line is how guys get hit on a comp audit, so we'd do it one way or the other, not in between.

Good for you

  • No equipment cost, no equipment risk
  • Steady paid work and access to bigger jobs than either of us lands alone
  • Someone else is chasing the customer for the check

Hard for you

  • It's a Second Nature job, not a Jimmy job
  • Paperwork up front to get set up clean

None of this is a take-it-or-leave-it. It's the three shapes I can see that don't leave one of us exposed. Look it over, tell me which one you'd lean toward, and we'll sit down and put real numbers on it.

— Doug
